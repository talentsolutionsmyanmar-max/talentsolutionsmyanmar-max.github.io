The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ko Min Khant has worked in agriculture for eight years — three years on his family's rice farm in Bago, then five years as a technician for an agricultural input company covering the delta region. He knows seed varieties, soil types, pesticide application, and farmer training. In early 2025, he heard that a major telecom operator's agricultural digital services division was hiring field coordinators to help launch a farmer advisory app. The salary was three times his current income. Ko Min Khant had no LinkedIn profile, no digital portfolio, no way to show his experience online. He sent his CV by email — a one-page document listing job titles and dates. The recruiter received 200 similar CVs. Meanwhile, a woman named Ma Su Myat with only two years of agricultural experience had a LinkedIn profile with photos of her field work, posts about crop management techniques, and recommendations from two previous supervisors. She was shortlisted. Ko Min Khant was not. He had more knowledge, more experience, and more to offer. But in the digital-first hiring world of 2026, he was invisible.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Myanmar agricultural workers believe that experience and relationships are sufficient for career advancement, and that creating a digital presence is unnecessary, self-promotional, or only for IT professionals. The cognitive distortion is Status Quo Bias — the belief that because agricultural hiring has always worked through personal networks and walk-in applications, it will continue to work that way. The cultural root cause is Myanmar's modesty norm — self-promotion feels deeply uncomfortable and is perceived as boastful, especially in agricultural communities where humility is valued. The devastating consequence: as Myanmar's agricultural sector modernizes and MNCs enter, hiring increasingly happens through digital channels. Workers without any digital presence are systematically excluded from the highest-paying 20% of agricultural jobs, regardless of their actual competence.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
Why digital visibility is now a survival skill in agriculture

Myanmar's agricultural sector is splitting into two economies. The first economy is traditional: local hiring, word-of-mouth, relationships, walk-in applications. Salaries in this economy are stagnant and shrinking against inflation. The second economy is digital-modern: LinkedIn recruitment, online applications, digital portfolios, MNC hiring processes. Salaries here are 2-5 times higher. The gap between these two economies is widening every month in 2026 as more international organizations and companies enter Myanmar agriculture. Taleb's asymmetric thinking applies perfectly: creating a LinkedIn profile costs zero kyat and 15 minutes. The potential upside is access to the entire upper tier of agricultural employment. The downside of not doing it is total invisibility to the fastest-growing employers. This is not about choosing digital over traditional. It is about adding a digital layer to your traditional strengths. The farmer who can both work the field and describe that work professionally online has an unfair advantage over everyone who can only do one.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
Overcoming the Myanmar modesty barrier to professional visibility

In Myanmar culture, talking about your achievements feels uncomfortable. The face-saving and modesty norms are deeply ingrained — especially in agricultural communities where communal identity matters more than individual recognition. Here is how to reframe digital visibility in a way that aligns with Myanmar values: you are not boasting about yourself. You are sharing knowledge that helps others. When you post about a farming technique you used, you help other farmers. When you describe a training you conducted, you honor the farmers who participated. When you share your results, you provide evidence that good practices work. Frame every post as giving, not taking. This completely dissolves the modesty barrier. Also remember: Myanmar agricultural employers have told us directly that they struggle to find qualified candidates because talented people are invisible online. By making yourself visible, you are solving their problem. That is service, not self-promotion.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Download the LinkedIn app from Play Store if you do not have it. Create a profile using your real name. For your headline, do not write just your job title. Write what you do and who it helps. Example: 'Agricultural Field Technician with experience in Delta region crop management and farmer training.' This headline is what recruiters see first in search results. Use Myanmar and English — many agricultural employers operate bilingually. Upload a clear photo of yourself in professional or field-appropriate clothing. This takes 10 minutes and immediately makes you visible to every agricultural recruiter in Myanmar.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

In your LinkedIn Experience section, describe each role using this formula: 'Action verb plus what you did plus measurable result.' Bad example: 'Worked with farmers on crop improvement.' Good example: 'Trained 150 smallholder farmers in Bago Region on integrated pest management techniques, contributing to an estimated 15% reduction in pesticide costs across participating farms.' Even if your numbers are estimates, quantified statements prove business thinking. Write at least 3 bullet points per role. Rewrite them until each one contains a number.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Starting today, use your phone camera to document your agricultural work. Take photos of fields you manage, training sessions you conduct, crops at different growth stages, any equipment you operate, and any certificates or awards you have. Create a folder on your phone called 'My Agri Portfolio.' These photos serve double purpose: they can be added to LinkedIn posts and they demonstrate your actual hands-on experience to any employer. Even simple photos taken with a basic Android camera are powerful proof of real work that a CV alone cannot provide.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Open LinkedIn and search for these organizations: 'a major conglomerate,' 'AWBA Group,' 'Proximity Designs,' 'a major multinational based in Yangon,' 'World Food Programme Myanmar,' 'FAO Myanmar,' 'a major telecom operator Myanmar,' 'Golden Land Myanmar.' Follow every one of them. Then search for 'agriculture jobs Myanmar' and turn on job alerts. Also on Facebook, follow 'Myanmar Agriculture Network,' 'Myanmar Agribusiness Forum,' and any group related to your specific crop or region. Engaging with these pages — even just liking and commenting thoughtfully — puts your name in front of the people who make hiring decisions.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Write your first LinkedIn post today. It does not need to be long. Use this template: 'I have been working in [your area] agriculture for [X years]. Today I learned [one thing from this training]. I believe Myanmar agriculture needs more [one thing you care about].' Add one photo from your work if you have one. This first post is terrifying for most Myanmar workers because of the modesty norm. But remember: this is not boasting. This is making yourself visible to employers who are actively searching for agricultural talent. One post per week builds a professional reputation that no paper CV can match.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

In Myanmar's agricultural sector, the professional who documents and shares their work digitally will consistently beat the more experienced professional who keeps everything in their head — because employers cannot hire what they cannot see.
