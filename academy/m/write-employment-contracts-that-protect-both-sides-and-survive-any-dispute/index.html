Write Employment Contracts That Protect Both Sides and Survive Any Dispute

The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ko Zaw Htet is an HR coordinator at a medium-sized trading company in Tamwe, Yangon. His boss asked him to prepare an employment letter for a new warehouse supervisor. Ko Zaw Htet typed a one-page letter in Myanmar stating the job title, salary, and start date. He printed it, both parties signed, and the new hire started work. Seven months later, the warehouse supervisor demanded overtime pay for weekend shifts. Ko Zaw Htet checked the contract. It said nothing about working hours, overtime policy, or termination terms. The company had no grounds to refuse the claim. The dispute went to the Township Labour Office. The mediator asked for the bilingual contract required under Myanmar Labor Law 2013. Ko Zaw Htet had only the one-page Myanmar letter. The mediator told him this document does not meet legal requirements. Your company has no enforceable contract. Ko Zaw Htet lost the case, lost his boss's trust, and nearly lost his job. A 30-minute investment in a proper contract would have prevented seven months of vulnerability.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

The number one mistake is treating contracts as a formality and copying a template without customizing it to the actual role. The cognitive distortion is Minimization: This is just paperwork, it does not really matter. The cultural root cause is Myanmar's relationship-first business culture where verbal trust between people is valued more than written terms. Many Myanmar managers believe that a good relationship makes a detailed contract unnecessary or even insulting, as if you do not trust the other person. The devastating consequence is that when the relationship breaks down, and in high-turnover Myanmar markets it will, there is no written foundation to resolve the dispute. The HR person who prepared the weak contract is held responsible for the entire financial and legal exposure.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
The 8 Non-Negotiable Elements of a Myanmar Employment Contract

Under the Employment and Skill Development Law 2013, a legally enforceable contract in Myanmar must be written and bilingual. But legal compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. A contract that actually protects your organization needs 8 elements. Job title and specific duties, not vague descriptions. Compensation including base salary, allowances, and payment schedule. Working hours including overtime calculation method. Probation period explicitly capped at 3 months with confirmation that full salary and SSB apply during probation. Termination conditions for both sides including notice period and severance. Confidentiality and non-disclosure terms. SSB enrollment confirmation with contribution percentages stated. Signature lines for both parties with dates. Here is the behavioral principle from Morgan Housel: raw talent in HR does not matter if you lack the discipline to get contracts right every single time. The best HR person is not the most charismatic. It is the most consistent. Build the habit of running every contract through your 8-point checklist before any signature.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
How to Implement Proper Contracts in a Myanmar Company That Has Never Used Them

Many Myanmar SMEs have operated for years on verbal agreements or one-page letters. You cannot walk in and demand they change overnight. That violates hierarchy and causes face-loss for the owner who built the company without formal contracts. Instead, use the asymmetric approach from Nassim Taleb's thinking: make a small move that has huge upside and near-zero downside. Draft the template yourself on your phone. Print one copy. Show it to your manager and say: I prepared this based on the 2013 law requirements. Would you like me to start using it for new hires? This costs you 30 minutes. The upside is legal protection, manager trust, and a reputation as someone who takes initiative. The downside is essentially zero because you are offering, not demanding. For existing employees, suggest a contract renewal process during annual reviews. Frame it as updating our records rather than admitting previous contracts were inadequate. In Myanmar business culture, the framing determines whether your idea is accepted or rejected.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open Google Docs on your phone. If you do not have the app, download it from Play Store now. It works on 3G and saves offline. Create a new document titled Employment Contract Template. Type these 8 section headers: Job Title and Description, Compensation and Benefits, Working Hours and Overtime, Probation Period with maximum 3 months noted, Termination Conditions, Confidentiality, SSB Enrollment Confirmation, and Signatures of Both Parties. You now have the skeleton of a legally compliant contract on your phone. Save it.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

Under each of the 8 section headers in your template, write 2 to 3 sentences of sample language. For example, under Probation Period write: The probation period shall be 3 months from the start date. During probation, the employee shall receive full salary and SSB coverage as required by law. Do not worry about perfect legal language yet. Write in plain Myanmar and plain English side by side. A bilingual draft that covers all sections is infinitely better than a polished single-language document that misses critical clauses.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Search on your phone browser for Myanmar employment contract template PDF. Download or screenshot the first 2 results. Compare them against your 8-section template. Mark which sections the downloaded templates are missing. Most free templates online are incomplete. By comparing, you are building the skill of contract auditing, which is exactly what senior HR managers at companies like a major conglomerate do during compliance reviews.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Open your phone notes app. Write a 3-sentence script you would use to explain to a manager why a detailed contract is necessary. Example: A strong contract is not about distrusting our employees. It is about protecting the company if a dispute reaches the Township Labour Office. The mediator's first question will be to see our written bilingual contract. Practice saying this script out loud twice. This prepares you for the real conversation.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Find one actual employment document from your current or previous job, whether it is a contract, offer letter, or appointment letter. Compare it against your 8-section checklist. Count how many sections are missing or incomplete. Write that number in your notes. This gap number is the exact measure of your company's legal vulnerability. If you are between jobs, use any job offer you have received and audit it the same way.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

A contract is not paperwork to start a job. A contract is insurance you buy before the disaster. The 30 minutes you spend writing a thorough contract saves the 30 days you would spend in a labor dispute you cannot win.
