When to Discuss Salary

CORE INSIGHT

The single most important rule about salary timing is this: the person who states a number first usually loses leverage. In a hiring process, your value in the employer's eyes increases with every interview round you survive. After the first screening call, you are one of many. After the second interview, you are one of few. After the final round, you may be the only one. Your salary leverage follows the same curve -- it peaks at the moment they decide they want you and have not yet locked in a number.

Most Myanmar professionals get this backwards. They think stating salary expectations early shows confidence and saves everyone's time. In reality, it caps your offer before the employer has fully experienced what you bring. The hiring manager who hears '500,000 MMK' in the first call anchors on that number even if their budget allows 700,000. You have given them a ceiling when you should have been building a floor.

The ideal moment to discuss salary in detail is after the employer has expressed clear intent to hire you -- typically at the offer stage or during a final-round discussion where they ask about your availability and start date. These are buying signals. When someone asks when you can start, they are no longer evaluating whether to hire you. They are planning how. That is when your number carries maximum weight.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

In Myanmar's job market, the salary question often comes earlier than global career advice suggests. Local companies -- especially SMEs in Hledan, Insein, or industrial zones -- frequently ask salary expectations in the very first phone screening. This is partly cultural efficiency and partly because many local HR teams use salary as a first-round filter. NGOs and INGOs tend to have fixed salary bands and will sometimes publish them, which changes the dynamic entirely. MNCs operating in Myanmar usually delay salary to the offer stage, following their global process.

Knowing which type of employer you are dealing with changes your strategy completely. For a local company that insists on a number in round one, you need a prepared range. For an MNC, you can comfortably defer. For NGOs with published bands, your negotiation is about grade placement, not the number itself. One strategy does not fit all three, and pretending otherwise is how Myanmar professionals leave money on the table every year.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most damaging mistake Myanmar professionals make is giving their current salary instead of their expected salary when asked early. This happens because of a cultural instinct toward honesty and humility -- saying your actual number feels safer than naming a higher one. But your current salary reflects your past employer's budget, not your market value. Recruiters who hear your current salary use it as an anchor, typically offering 10-15% above it, even when the role's budget is 30-50% higher. The consequence is systematic underpayment that compounds across your entire career.

ACTION STEPS

  1. Before any interview, go to MyJobs.com.mm or JobNet.com.mm, search your exact job title filtered to Yangon, and record the salary range shown in at least 5 current postings. This is your market data.
  1. Prepare two responses for the salary question: one for early-stage ('I am flexible within the market range of X to Y and would love to discuss after learning more about the full role') and one for offer-stage ('Based on my experience and the market, I am looking for X').
  1. Identify whether your target employer is local, NGO, or MNC, and adjust your timing expectation. Ask the recruiter or agent about the interview process stages before your first call.
  1. Practice saying your salary range out loud -- in English and Myanmar -- five times. The number must leave your mouth without hesitation, filler words, or a rising tone that turns it into a question.
  1. If asked for your current salary specifically, redirect: 'My current package is structured differently, so I would prefer to discuss expectations for this role. I am targeting the range of X to Y based on the scope here.'
