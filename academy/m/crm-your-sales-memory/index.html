CRM — Your Sales Memory

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management, but think of it as your sales memory — the system that remembers everything so you do not have to. Every conversation, every promise, every follow-up date, every preference, every objection — stored in one place, accessible anytime.

Why you need a CRM: the human brain can reliably track about 7 active relationships. A working salesperson in Myanmar handles 20 to 100 prospects at different stages simultaneously. Without a system, critical follow-ups get missed, promises get forgotten, and opportunities die silently. The number one reason salespeople lose deals in Myanmar is not price, not competition — it is forgetting to follow up.

Starting simple — the notebook CRM. If you are beginning your sales career, start with a physical notebook divided into 5 sections matching your pipeline stages: Prospect, Contact, Qualify, Propose, Close. Write every interaction with a date. Review it every morning. This costs nothing and works better than no system at all. The habit of recording matters more than the tool.

The spreadsheet CRM. When your notebook gets too crowded (usually around 30 active prospects), move to a Google Sheet or Excel file with columns: Name, Company, Phone, Stage, Last Contact Date, Next Action, Next Action Date, Notes. Sort by Next Action Date every morning. This becomes your daily to-do list. Share it with your manager for coaching conversations.

Professional CRM tools. When your team grows beyond 3 salespeople or your prospect list exceeds 100, invest in a proper CRM. Popular options in Myanmar include HubSpot (free tier available, excellent for small teams), Zoho CRM (affordable, multilingual), and Salesforce (enterprise-grade, expensive). The best CRM is the one your team actually uses — a simple tool used consistently beats a sophisticated tool used sporadically.

What to track in your CRM: contact details (name, phone, Viber, email, company, position), every interaction (calls, meetings, Viber messages — with dates and notes), the prospect's pain points (what problem are they trying to solve), decision-makers (who else influences the buying decision), competitor information (who else are they considering), deal value and stage, next action with specific date. The discipline of entering this data after every interaction takes 2 minutes and saves hours of confusion later.

CRM habits that transform results: update within 1 hour of every customer interaction (not at the end of the week when you have forgotten details). Set follow-up reminders for every open conversation — never rely on memory. Review your CRM dashboard every Monday morning to plan your week. Clean dead leads monthly — remove contacts who have been unresponsive for 60+ days to keep your pipeline honest.

The CRM mindset shift: it is not administrative burden. It is your competitive advantage. The salesperson who remembers that a prospect's daughter just started university, or that they mentioned supply chain issues last quarter, or that they prefer calls over Viber — that salesperson wins. The CRM remembers so you can focus on connecting.
