The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Thida got hired as an admin assistant at a trading company near Bayint Naung. During her interview, the manager said the probation period was six months. Ma Thida was so relieved to get the job that she agreed instantly. For five months, she worked 10-hour days, took on extra tasks, and received no overtime pay. The manager told her benefits and overtime would start 'after probation.' On the last week of the fifth month, the manager said her performance was not satisfactory and terminated her. No notice. No compensation. No SSB card — because the company said they only register employees after probation ends. Ma Thida lost five months of labor with no legal protection, no SSB contributions, and no documentation. She later discovered through a Facebook labour rights group that the maximum probation period under Myanmar law is three months. The extra two months were illegal. The refusal to register SSB during probation was also illegal. She had rights from day one that she never knew existed. By the time she learned, it was too late to recover what she lost.

What do you already believe?

Think carefully before answering: Do you believe that during your probation period, your employer has no obligation to register you for SSB or pay overtime? Do you believe the employer can set any probation length they want — 6 months, 12 months? If you answered yes to either question, you have been operating with a belief that costs you money and legal protection every single day of your probation.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Myanmar workers accept any probation terms the employer states — including illegal durations of 6 or 12 months — and believe they have no rights until probation ends. The cognitive distortion is All-or-Nothing Thinking: 'I am either a real employee with rights OR a probation worker with no rights.' The cultural root cause is deep hierarchy respect — a new employee feels they have the lowest status and therefore cannot question any term set by management. The devastating consequence is months of unprotected labor where workers receive no SSB, no overtime, no leave, and can be terminated without notice — all of which violates Myanmar law but goes unchallenged because the worker does not know the boundary between legal flexibility and illegal exploitation.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
What the Law Actually Says About Your Probation Period

Under Myanmar's Employment and Skill Development Law 2013, the maximum probation period is 3 months. Not 6 months. Not 'until the employer decides.' Three months. Any probation period exceeding this is a violation of the law. During probation, you are a legal employee. This means: your employer must register you for SSB and begin contributions, you are entitled to overtime pay for hours exceeding the standard workweek, you are entitled to public holidays and weekly rest days, and if terminated, the employer must have reasonable grounds. Probation is not a legal grey zone — it is a defined evaluation period with full worker protections. Think about this through the lens of asymmetric risk. The cost to you of not knowing your probation rights is enormous — months of lost SSB contributions, unpaid overtime, and vulnerability to unfair termination. The cost of learning these rights is 15 minutes of your time right now. This is the most lopsided trade you will ever make in your career. The process of learning your rights is itself the victory, regardless of whether you need to use them tomorrow or next year.

Navigating Probation in Myanmar's Real Workplace Culture

The challenge in Myanmar is not the law — it is the culture around the law. New employees feel intense pressure to be grateful, quiet, and compliant. Questioning probation terms feels like biting the hand that feeds you. Here is how to navigate this reality. First, ask about probation terms during the offer stage, not after you start. Before you accept, say: 'Could you clarify the probation period and which benefits begin on my start date?' This frames you as thorough, not confrontational. Second, if you discover your probation exceeds 3 months after starting, do not publicly challenge your manager. Instead, raise it with HR privately and frame it as a compliance concern: 'I noticed my probation is set at 6 months. I read that the legal maximum is 3 months — I want to make sure we are compliant.' Third, document everything. Save your offer letter, any messages about probation terms, and your start date. At companies like a major multinational based in Yangon or a major conglomerate, probation is standardized at 3 months with full benefits from day one. Use these MNC standards as your benchmark for what normal looks like. If a local company offers dramatically worse terms, that information tells you how they will treat you as a permanent employee.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open your phone notes and create a new entry titled 'Probation Rights Checklist.' Write these 5 items: (1) Maximum probation is 3 months by law, (2) SSB registration must happen from the start of employment not after probation, (3) Overtime pay applies during probation, (4) Public holidays and weekly rest days apply during probation, (5) Termination during probation still requires reasonable grounds. Save this note. Review it before starting any new job.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

If you are currently on probation or recently completed probation, open your phone calendar and count the exact number of days from your start date. If your probation exceeded or is set to exceed 3 months (approximately 90 days), take a screenshot of any communication — offer letter, Viber message, email — that states the probation duration. Save it in your Work Documents folder. This is evidence if you ever need to challenge the terms.

Action 3

Search Facebook groups for 'Myanmar labour rights' or 'Myanmar worker rights' and find posts from other workers who challenged illegal probation periods. Read at least 2 stories. Notice what steps they took: many visited the Township Labour Office or contacted organizations like the Confederation of Trade Unions Myanmar. Save useful posts. Learning from others' real experiences is faster than reading the law alone and shows you what is actually possible in practice.

Action 4

Open Google and search 'Myanmar Employment and Skill Development Law 2013 probation' or search in Myanmar language. Find and read the specific section about probation duration. Action 5

Draft a message in your Viber or Messenger drafts that you can use when starting any new job. Write: 'Mingalabar, thank you for this opportunity. I would like to kindly confirm the probation period duration and understand which benefits, including SSB registration and overtime pay, apply during the probation period. I want to make sure I understand all terms correctly from the beginning.' This message is respectful, professional, and legally informed. It signals to the employer that you are a serious worker who knows the law — and employers who comply with the law will respect you for it.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

Probation does not mean you have no rights — it means the employer is evaluating you while the law still fully protects you. The three-month legal maximum exists specifically to prevent companies from using endless probation as a tool to avoid their obligations to you.
