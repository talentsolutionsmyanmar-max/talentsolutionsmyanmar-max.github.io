The Sales Pipeline — Your Daily GPS

Your sales pipeline is a visual map of every potential deal you are working on, organized by stage. Think of it as your daily GPS — without it, you are driving blind. You might be busy, but you do not know if you are moving toward revenue or just moving.

A basic sales pipeline has 5 stages: Prospect (people who might need your product), Contact (you have spoken to them at least once), Qualify (they have a real need, budget, and authority to buy), Propose (you have sent a quotation or presented your solution), and Close (they said yes or no). Every potential deal sits in one of these stages at any given time.

The power of the pipeline is visibility. When you look at your pipeline on Monday morning, you should immediately know: How many prospects did I add last week? How many moved forward a stage? How many are stuck? Where is the bottleneck? In Myanmar sales environments, the most common bottleneck is between Qualify and Propose — salespeople have many warm contacts but delay sending quotations because they fear rejection or want the relationship to be "perfect" first. The quotation IS the next step in the relationship.

Daily pipeline habits that separate top performers from average sellers: First, update your pipeline every evening before you leave work. This takes 5 minutes and gives you clarity for the next morning. Second, add at least 3 new prospects per week — if you only work existing deals, your pipeline dries up the moment you close or lose them. Third, move stale deals out. If a prospect has been in the same stage for more than 30 days without any response, they are not a prospect — they are a hope. Remove them or restart from scratch.

You do not need expensive CRM software to start. A simple notebook with 5 columns works. A spreadsheet on your phone works. The tool does not matter. The discipline of updating it daily matters. As you grow, a proper CRM system will save you hours per week, but even the best CRM is useless if you do not input data consistently.

Pipeline math is your forecasting tool. If you know that on average, 1 out of every 5 qualified prospects becomes a customer, and your average deal is worth 500,000 MMK, then you need 25 qualified prospects in your pipeline to hit a target of 2,500,000 MMK. This simple calculation tells you exactly how much prospecting activity you need each week. No guessing. No anxiety. Just math and action.

Review your pipeline every Friday. Count: How many deals entered? How many advanced? How many closed (won or lost)? How many were removed? These four numbers tell the complete story of your sales week. Over time, patterns emerge that help you improve at every stage.

Real-World Scenario

Ko Min Thu was the busiest sales rep at Thura Group — 50 client visits per week, always on the phone, never at his desk. But his monthly revenue was the lowest on the team. His manager pulled up the CRM and showed him: "You have 200 leads, but zero are past the proposal stage. You are starting new conversations every day but never finishing any." Ko Min Thu was running a marathon by sprinting 100 meters in every direction.

## Common Mistake

A sales pipeline without stages is just a list of names. The critical mistake is treating all leads equally — giving the same attention to someone who just heard your name as to someone who has seen your proposal and is ready to decide. Your pipeline must have clear stages (Lead → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Closed) and you must know how many prospects are in each stage at all times.

## Action Steps

  1. Draw your pipeline on paper with 5 columns: New Leads, Qualified, Proposal Sent, Negotiation, Closed Won. Place every current prospect in the correct column. If 80% are in "New Leads," you have a follow-up problem.
  1. Set a daily rule: spend 60% of your time on prospects in Proposal or Negotiation stages (close to money), 30% on qualifying new leads, and 10% on cold outreach.
  1. Review your pipeline every Friday. For each prospect, answer: "What is the next specific action, and when?" If you cannot answer, that prospect is dead — remove them and focus on live opportunities.

## Reflection Question

Calculate your current conversion rate: how many initial meetings does it take to close one deal? If you do not know the number, that is the first problem to solve. You cannot improve what you do not measure.
