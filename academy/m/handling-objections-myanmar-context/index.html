Handling Objections — Myanmar Context

Objections are not obstacles — they are buying signals in disguise. When a prospect raises an objection, they are telling you they are interested enough to think critically about your offer. A prospect who is not interested does not bother objecting. They just say "I will think about it" and disappear. An objection means engagement.

In Myanmar business culture, objections are often expressed indirectly. Understanding the hidden meaning behind common phrases will transform your closing rate:

"The price is too high" usually means one of three things: they do not understand the full value yet, they are comparing you to a cheaper alternative, or they genuinely cannot afford it. Your job is to discover which one. Ask: "Compared to what?" This simple question reveals their reference point and gives you the information you need to respond intelligently.

"I need to discuss with my partner/boss" is often genuine in Myanmar where decisions involve multiple stakeholders. Instead of feeling blocked, say: "That makes sense. Would it be helpful if I prepared a one-page summary for them? What concerns do you think they might have?" This shows respect for their process AND keeps you involved in the conversation.

"We are happy with our current supplier" is a loyalty statement, not a rejection. Respond with: "That is great — having a reliable supplier is important. I am not asking you to switch. Would you be open to a small trial order so you have a backup option?" In Myanmar business, backup relationships are valued because supply chain disruptions are common.

"Not now, maybe later" is the most common soft rejection in Myanmar. Ask: "I understand timing matters. What would need to change for this to become a priority?" Their answer tells you when to follow up and with what message.

The objection handling framework: LAER. Listen completely — do not interrupt. Acknowledge their concern genuinely — "I understand why you feel that way." Explore the real issue — ask questions to get beneath the surface objection. Respond with a relevant solution — not a generic pitch, but a specific answer to their specific concern.

Common mistake in Myanmar sales: arguing with the objection. When a prospect says "too expensive," many salespeople immediately start discounting. This destroys value and trains customers to always negotiate. Instead, build value first. Explain the return on investment. Show testimonials from similar customers. Only adjust price as a last resort, and always tie discounts to something in return — a larger order, a longer commitment, or a referral.

Practice handling the 5 most common objections in your industry until your responses feel natural, not scripted. Role-play with colleagues. Record yourself and listen back. The salesperson who handles objections smoothly projects confidence, and confidence closes deals.

Real-World Scenario

Ma Aye Aye pitched Thura Group's new inventory software to a retail chain owner. He said: "It is too expensive." She panicked and offered a 30% discount on the spot. She got the sale but her commission was slashed and her manager was furious. Later she learned: when the owner said "too expensive," he actually meant "I do not understand the value yet." If she had asked "What are you comparing the price to?" she would have discovered he was comparing software to a notebook — and could have shown how the software would save him 10x the cost.

## Common Mistake

The biggest objection-handling mistake is treating every objection as a rejection. In Myanmar business culture, objections are often invitations to continue the conversation — not signals to stop. "Too expensive" means "convince me." "I need to think about it" means "I have concerns you have not addressed." "We already have a supplier" means "What can you offer that they cannot?" The worst response to any objection is to immediately lower your price.

## Action Steps

  1. Learn the "Feel-Felt-Found" framework for Myanmar context: "I understand how you feel. Other clients in Mandalay felt the same way initially. What they found was that [specific benefit] saved them [specific amount] within [timeframe]."
  1. When you hear "too expensive," never drop the price first. Instead ask: "Compared to what?" or "What budget did you have in mind?" This reveals whether the objection is about price or about value understanding.
  1. Role-play the 5 most common objections with a colleague. Practice until your responses feel natural. Objection handling is a skill that improves with repetition, not inspiration.

## Reflection Question

Think about the last sale you lost. What objection did the customer raise? How did you respond? Now, knowing what you know, how would you respond differently?
