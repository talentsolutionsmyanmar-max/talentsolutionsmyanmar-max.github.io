The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Ei Phyu works as a logistics assistant at a consumer goods distributor in Insein Township. Her company cannot afford SAP or Oracle supply chain software. Her boss tracks everything in a notebook. When the notebook gets wet, gets lost, or pages tear out, weeks of data disappear. Ma Ei Phyu asked if they could use a computer system. Her boss said the company could not afford one. For six months, Ma Ei Phyu accepted this. Then her cousin who works at a a major telecom operator distribution center showed her something on his phone: a Google Sheet that tracked every delivery, every return, every damage report — with automatic calculations and color coding for late shipments. His phone cost 150,000 kyats. The app was free. Ma Ei Phyu spent two evenings learning Google Sheets by watching YouTube tutorials. Within one month, she had digitized her company's entire delivery tracking. Her boss was stunned. Three months later, when a a mobile payments company regional office needed a logistics coordinator, Ma Ei Phyu's boss personally recommended her. She got the job at twice her previous salary. The tool was free. The effort was two evenings. The return was her entire career trajectory.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Myanmar workers believe that digital supply chain tools require expensive software, computers, and formal IT training. This is a classic All-or-Nothing cognitive distortion — believing that if you cannot have the perfect professional system, the basic free tools are worthless. The cultural root cause is Myanmar's education system, which teaches that legitimate skills come from formal courses and certificates, not self-taught phone skills. Workers wait for their company to provide systems rather than building their own. The devastating career consequence is that while they wait, workers who taught themselves Google Sheets, basic data analysis, and digital tracking on their phones are being hired and promoted over them. At MNC employers like a major multinational based in Yangon and an established Myanmar bank supply chain divisions, digital literacy on basic tools is now a minimum hiring requirement — not a bonus skill. Workers who cannot demonstrate basic digital tracking are eliminated before the interview even begins.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
Your Phone Is More Powerful Than Your Boss's Notebook

Here is a fact that changes everything: a free Google Sheet on a 150,000 kyat Android phone can do automatic calculations, sort data, create charts, be accessed from anywhere, be shared with anyone, and never get lost in a flood. A paper notebook can do none of these things. Yet across Myanmar, the majority of supply chain tracking still happens on paper — or worse, in someone's memory. This is not a technology problem. Myanmar has widespread smartphone access. This is a belief problem: workers believe that 'real' digital tools are expensive, and that phone apps are for Facebook and Viber only. MJ DeMarco's progression framework applies here: the employment-level worker uses whatever tools the boss provides. The self-directed professional builds their own tools. You do not need permission to open Google Sheets. You do not need a budget approval. You do not need IT support. You need fifteen minutes and the willingness to type your first formula. That is the asymmetric opportunity Taleb describes: tiny effort, massive career impact. The risk of learning is zero. The risk of not learning is being permanently outcompeted by workers who did.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
Building Your Digital Reputation in Myanmar's Job Market

In Myanmar's 2026 job market, the ability to show — not just tell — your skills is the difference between being shortlisted and being ignored. When you apply to a logistics coordinator role at a major multinational based in Yangon, a major conglomerate, or any growing Myanmar company, imagine showing the interviewer your phone: 'Here is my inventory tracking system. Here is my delivery tracker with automatic delay calculations. Here is my supplier scorecard. Here is my waste log with monthly cost estimates.' No other candidate will do this. They will talk about their experience in vague terms. You will show a working system. This is your digital portfolio, and it costs zero kyats. Here is the critical principle: start ugly, improve gradually. Your first Google Sheet will not be beautiful. Your first formula will probably have errors. That is perfect. The process of building, breaking, and fixing your own tools teaches you more than any training course. Celebrate every sheet you create. Celebrate every formula you fix. The beautiful spreadsheet comes later. The habit of digital tracking starts today. Share your tools with colleagues via WhatsApp links — this builds your reputation as the tech-capable person in your workplace, which is exactly the reputation that leads to promotions and recommendations.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Install Google Sheets app from Play Store if you do not have it already (it is free and works on 3G). Open it and create your first sheet titled 'Delivery Tracker.' In Row 1, type these headers: Shipment ID, Supplier, Product, Sent Date, Expected Date, Actual Arrival Date, Delay Days. In the Delay Days column, you will later learn to put a formula. For now, just create the structure and enter three recent deliveries from memory. You have just built your first digital tracking system. It took five minutes. That is five minutes more than most supply chain workers in Myanmar have invested in digital tools.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

Open YouTube and search 'Google Sheets beginner tutorial Myanmar' or 'Google Sheets beginner tutorial' if Myanmar language is not available. Watch one video under fifteen minutes. Focus specifically on learning two things: how to type a SUM formula to add numbers automatically, and how to use basic formatting like bold headers and colored cells. Practice both on your Delivery Tracker sheet immediately after watching. Do not watch passively — pause and practice on your phone. The physical act of typing formulas into your phone builds muscle memory that reading about it never will. Duke's principle: celebrate completing the tutorial, not getting every formula right.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Open Google Forms app (free from Play Store). Create a new form titled 'Damage Report.' Add these questions: Date (date field), Product Name (short answer), Quantity Damaged (number), Type of Damage (multiple choice: Wet, Crushed, Expired, Torn Packaging, Other), Probable Cause (short answer), Photo (file upload). Share the form link via WhatsApp to yourself. Open the link on your phone browser to test it. You have just created a digital reporting system that most Myanmar warehouses do not have. When responses come in, they automatically populate a Google Sheet — giving you a database of damage incidents without any manual data entry.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Go back to your Google Sheet from Module 1 (Supplier Scorecard) or create a new one. Add a column called 'Rating 1-5' and another called 'Last Contact Date.' Rate each supplier honestly from 1 to 5 based on reliability. Update the Last Contact Date. Now tap 'Share' and select 'Copy Link.' Paste this link into a WhatsApp message to yourself. This link is your portable supplier database. You can share it with any future employer in a job interview. When an interviewer at a major conglomerate or an established Myanmar bank asks 'how do you evaluate suppliers?' you can show them an actual working system on your phone. That is more impressive than any certificate.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Create one master Google Sheet called 'My Supply Chain Dashboard.' Make four tabs at the bottom: Inventory (from Module 2), Delivery Tracking (from this module), Supplier Scorecard (from Module 3), and Waste Log (from Module 4). You now have a complete, interconnected, free, phone-based supply chain management system. Spend ten minutes reviewing all four tabs. Notice how they connect — suppliers affect delivery times, delivery delays create inventory problems, inventory problems create waste. This is systems thinking made tangible on your phone. Take a screenshot of your dashboard. Save it. This screenshot is proof of what you built, and the beginning of a digital portfolio that separates you from every other candidate.
