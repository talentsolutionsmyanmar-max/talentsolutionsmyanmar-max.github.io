The Story

Ma Thiri has her Calibrated Search Brief from U Kyaw Zin: route-sales team leader, two years managing vans-dealers-daily targets, Yangon. Feeling professional, she does what everyone does: posts "URGENT HIRING — Sales Manager, good salary!" into three Facebook job groups. By evening she has 214 comments — "interested po", CVs for accountants, a photo of a puppy — and not one route-sales leader. Across the office, Ma Sandar, a senior TRM recruiter, fills the same kind of role in two days without posting anything. Her secret is not effort. It is that she never shouts into the crowd. She hunts: she searched ReferTRM's own candidate pool with three title variants before lunch, called the two best route-sales supervisors she knows and asked each "who's the best person you've ever worked with on a route?", and by evening had a shortlist of four names — three of whom were not looking for a job at all. The best candidates are almost never in the 214 comments. They are working a route right now, and they answer to a good search, a good name, or a good question.

The teachingCard 2 of 8
Why the best people don't apply

In Myanmar the strongest candidates usually already have jobs. They don't scroll job groups; their next job finds them through someone they trust. These are PASSIVE CANDIDATES, and reaching them is what sourcing means. Second truth: you are not starting from zero. ReferTRM's own candidate pool — thousands of profiles with behavioral and skills data — is your first hunting ground, and it is the one no outside recruiter has. Searching it well is literally the platform's edge in your hands. Third truth: a post reaches whoever happens to scroll by; a SEARCH reaches exactly who you specify. That is why this module is about strings, not posters.

The teachingCard 3 of 8
The Four Hunting Grounds (work them in order)
  1. THE POOL YOU OWN — ReferTRM's candidate database, always first. Search with title variants from your brief, filter by the must-haves, read the behavioral signals. One good pool search often ends the hunt before it starts. 2) THE REFERRAL WEB — talent mapping. Call the two or three best people in that role you know (or your last good placement) and ask THE question: "Who's the best person you've worked with on this kind of work?" Names given by strong performers are pre-screened by reality. This is how Ma Sandar found three non-lookers in an afternoon. 3) PLATFORMS, SEARCHED — LinkedIn (thin in Myanmar but strong for MNC/senior), Facebook groups (SEARCH them with keywords — don't post), Viber communities, university alumni pages. Treat each like a database, not a noticeboard. 4) THE QUIET MARKET — direct phone sourcing for people with no internet footprint (real for factory, distribution, and upcountry roles): the dealer who knows every route supervisor, the township contact, the teashop network. Old-school, still decisive in Myanmar.
The teachingCard 4 of 8
The Five String Rules (Boolean, plainly)

A search string is a sentence a database understands. Five rules cover ninety percent of it: 1) OR the synonyms — the same job hides under many titles: ("route sales" OR "area sales" OR "distribution supervisor" OR "sales supervisor"). Put ORs inside parentheses. 2) AND the must-haves — AND narrows: (…titles…) AND Yangon AND FMCG. Every AND is a wall; only add walls you mean. 3) NOT the noise — exclude what floods you: NOT ("hiring" OR "vacancy" OR "apply now") clears job ads from results; NOT retail clears shop-floor profiles when you need route people. 4) "QUOTES" for exact phrases — "route sales" finds the phrase; without quotes you find every page with 'route' and 'sales' separately. Quotes = precision; parentheses = grouping. They are different tools. 5) COMBINE with context — add geography, industry, or a company: site:linkedin.com "route sales" Yangon FMCG — the site: trick (X-ray search) lets a normal search engine search inside a platform. DRILLS (do all five — the module's hands-on core, from Jason's drill canon): D1 Name three search engines beyond Google you could source with. D2 Write a string that finds route-sales leaders but EXCLUDES job ads. D3 Write a string for "graduates of Yangon University of Economics who work at an FMCG company". D4 Explain what changes if you drop the quotes from "route sales". D5 Take your M1 brief and write its three-variant title OR-set with one geo AND one industry wall.

The teachingCard 5 of 8
Hunting with courtesy

Cold-approaching a working person must protect face — theirs and their current employer's. Open soft and private: "A senior person recommended you as one of the best at route work. I have something that may fit you — is now a bad time?" Never say who you're hiring away from in public comments; never pressure a passive candidate to 'be grateful' for the approach. In talent-mapping calls, thank the referrer and keep their name out of it unless they agree. And log every sourced name into the pool honestly — the platform's data dignity rules apply to sourcing, not just submitting.

The common mistakeCard 6 of 8
The mistake almost everyone makes

Posting and waiting — then judging the market by the 214 comments. Close cousins: searching ONE title only (the OR-set is the fix); building a wall of ANDs so tall nobody passes; and skipping the ReferTRM pool to hunt outside first. If you remember one sentence: search the pool you own with the string your brief wrote, before you touch the open market.

လက်ရှိအလုပ်ရှိပြီးသားသူတစ်ယောက်ကို ဆက်သွယ်တဲ့အခါ စကားစပုံက အရမ်းအရေးကြီးပါတယ်။ သူ့လက်ရှိအလုပ်ကို မထိခိုက်စေရဘူး။ ကိုယ်ဆက်သွယ်လိုက်တာကြောင့် သူ့ဘက်က မသက်မသာဖြစ်သွားတာမျိုးလည်း မဖြစ်စေရပါဘူး။ အမြဲ Private Message ဒါမှမဟုတ် Private Call နဲ့စပါ။ ဥပမာ — “အစ်ကိုက route-sales ပိုင်းမှာ အလုပ်ကောင်းကောင်းလုပ်နိုင်တဲ့သူလို့ သိတဲ့သူတစ်ယောက်က ညွှန်းပေးထားလို့ ဆက်သွယ်တာပါ။ အစ်ကို့အတွေ့အကြုံနဲ့ကိုက်နိုင်တဲ့ အခွင့်အရေးတစ်ခုရှိပါတယ်။ အခုစကားပြောဖို့အဆင်မပြေသေးရင် အဆင်ပြေတဲ့အချိန်ကိုပြောပေးလို့ရပါတယ်” လို့ပြောနိုင်ပါတယ်။ Public Comment ထဲမှာ ဘယ် Company ကခေါ်ချင်တာလဲ၊ ဘယ်သူကညွှန်းတာလဲဆိုတာ မရေးပါနဲ့။ Passive Candidate ကို အလုပ်ပြောင်းအောင်ဖိအားမပေးပါနဲ့။ Talent Mapping လုပ်တဲ့အခါ ညွှန်းပေးသူကိုကျေးဇူးတင်ပါ။ သူ့ခွင့်ပြုချက်မရဘဲ သူ့နာမည်ကို Candidate ဆီမှာ မသုံးပါနဲ့။ ရလာတဲ့ Candidate Name တွေကို ReferTRM Pool ထဲမှာ ဘယ်ကရလာတာလဲဆိုတာ မှန်မှန်ကန်ကန်မှတ်တမ်းတင်ပါ။ Candidate Data ကို လေးစားပြီးသုံးရမယ့် ReferTRM ရဲ့စည်းကမ်းက Sourcing လုပ်တဲ့အခါလည်း အတူတူပဲဖြစ်ပါတယ်။

Recruiter တွေအများဆုံးလုပ်မိတဲ့အမှားက Job Post တင်ပြီး လျှောက်လာမယ့်သူကိုပဲ ထိုင်စောင့်နေတာပါ။ Comment 214 ခုရလာတာကိုမြင်ပြီး “Market ထဲမှာ Candidate တွေအများကြီးရှိတယ်” လို့ အလွယ်တကူဆုံးဖြတ်မိတာလည်း အမှားပါ။ နောက်ထပ်အမှားတွေက Job title တစ်မျိုးတည်းနဲ့ပဲရှာတာ၊ AND တွေအများကြီးထည့်ပြီး ကိုယ်လိုချင်တဲ့လူတွေကိုတောင် Search Result ထဲမဝင်နိုင်အောင်ပိတ်မိတာ၊ ReferTRM Pool ကိုမရှာဘဲ အပြင် Market ကိုတန်းသွားတာတွေပါ။ မှတ်ထားရမယ့်အချက်က ရိုးရှင်းပါတယ်။ အပြင်ကိုမထွက်ခင် M1 က search brief ကိုပြန်ကြည့်ပါ။ ပြီးရင် ကိုယ့်လက်ထဲမှာရှိတဲ့ candidate pool ကိုအရင်ရှာပါ။
