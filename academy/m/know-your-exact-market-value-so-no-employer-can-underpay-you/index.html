Know Your Exact Market Value So No Employer Can Underpay You

The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Hnin Si works as an admin assistant at a trading company near Bayint Naung wholesale market in Yangon. When she was offered a new position at a logistics company in Hlaing Tharyar industrial zone, the HR manager asked her expected salary. Ma Hnin Si froze. She had no idea what admin assistants with three years of experience actually earn in logistics. She panicked and said 350,000 kyats because that was what she currently made. She got the job. Three months later, she discovered during a tea break conversation that her colleague — hired the same month, same role, less experience — negotiated 450,000 kyats. That is 100,000 kyats per month Ma Hnin Si lost. Over one year, that is 1,200,000 kyats. Not because she lacked skill. Not because the company refused to pay more. Because she walked into a negotiation without knowing the one number that matters most: what the market actually pays for her work.

The flipCard 2 of 12
What do you already believe?

When someone asks you 'What is your expected salary?' — what number comes to your mind first, and why that specific number? Think honestly: is it based on what you currently earn, what you need to pay bills, or what you have researched the market actually pays? Most people discover their number comes from emotion, not evidence.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

The most devastating mistake is naming your expected salary based on your current salary instead of market rate. The cognitive distortion driving this is anchoring bias — your brain locks onto the most familiar number, which is your current paycheck, and treats it as the starting point for all future negotiations. The cultural root cause in Myanmar is gratitude conditioning: workers are taught to feel thankful for any employment, so asking for significantly more than what you already receive feels greedy or disrespectful to the previous employer who gave you a chance. The consequence is career-long underpayment. Each job builds on the last salary, creating a compounding poverty trap where you fall further behind market rate with every move. After five job changes, you could be earning 30-40% below what your skills command.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
Why your current salary is the worst anchor for your next salary

Your brain uses shortcuts called heuristics. One of the most powerful is the anchoring effect: whatever number enters your mind first becomes the gravitational center of all your calculations. When an employer asks your expected salary, your brain instantly reaches for the most available number — your current pay. This is dangerous because your current pay reflects your past negotiation skill, not your current market value. In Myanmar's 2026 economy, with inflation above 20% and significant talent gaps from outmigration, many roles now pay 20-40% more than they did two years ago. If you anchor to your old salary, you are negotiating against yourself before the employer even makes their move. The process that protects you is research before conversation. Never enter any salary discussion — formal or informal — without first completing market research. This is not about being greedy. This is about being informed. Morgan Housel teaches that financial outcomes depend more on behavior than intelligence. The behavior here is simple: research first, speak second. Every time.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
How to use market data in Myanmar salary conversations without losing face

In Myanmar workplace culture, directly stating 'I want more money' can feel aggressive and risks face-loss for both parties. The solution is to let the data speak instead of your desire. When asked about salary expectations, use this framework: Reference, Range, Respect. First, Reference external data: 'Based on current market postings for this role in Yangon, the range I have seen is...' This shifts the authority from your personal demand to market reality. Second, state a Range, not a single number: always give a range where your ideal salary is the bottom number. Third, show Respect for the employer's structure: 'I am open to discussing how this fits within your compensation framework.' This approach works at both local Myanmar companies where hierarchy is strict and at MNCs like a major multinational based in Yangon or a major telecom operator where structured salary bands exist. You are not demanding. You are demonstrating that you are a professional who has done research. Employers at every level respect preparation more than they respect passive acceptance.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open your phone browser and go to MyJobs.com.mm right now. Search your current job title. Screenshot at least five job postings that show salary ranges. Save these screenshots in a dedicated phone folder called 'My Market Value.' If MyJobs does not show salary for your role, try work.com.mm and JobNet.com.mm. Note the minimum, maximum, and most common salary listed. This folder becomes your negotiation evidence file that you will reference in every future salary conversation.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

Open Facebook and search for Myanmar HR professional groups such as 'Myanmar HR Community' or 'HR Professional Myanmar Network.' Join at least two groups. Use the search function within each group to find posts about salary surveys or compensation discussions for your industry. Screenshot any relevant salary data. Many HR professionals in Myanmar share annual salary survey results from Antal International or John Clements Myanmar in these groups for free.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Open the Notes app on your phone. Write down your current salary. Below it, write the market range you just discovered. Calculate the gap in kyats per month and per year. Then write one sentence in this exact format: 'Based on current market data from MyJobs and industry salary surveys, professionals in [your role] with [your years] experience in [your industry] in Yangon earn between [low range] and [high range] kyats per month.' This is your market-value anchor sentence. Memorize it.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Record yourself on your phone's voice recorder saying your market-value anchor sentence three times. Listen to the playback. Notice if your voice drops when you say the higher number — this reveals your discomfort with claiming your value. Practice until you can say the full range clearly and steadily without your voice wavering. This is not vanity practice. MNC recruiters at companies like an established Myanmar bank, a mobile payments company, and a major conglomerate are trained to detect hesitation in salary discussions.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Send a WhatsApp message to two trusted friends or former colleagues in similar roles. Ask them this exact question: 'I am doing some career research. For someone with my experience in our field, what salary range do you think companies are offering right now?' Do not reveal your current salary. Their answers give you additional market data points and also practice asking about money in a low-stakes environment before you face a real employer.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

You do not get paid what you deserve — you get paid what you can prove the market pays. Deserving more is an emotion. Knowing the number is a weapon.

Think it overCard 12 of 12
How did this change what you knew?

Before this module, what was the number you would have said if asked your expected salary tomorrow? After doing the research steps, what number or range would you say now? Write down both. The gap between those two numbers represents the money you would have left on the table by relying on instinct instead of evidence.

Expected salary မေးခံရသောအခါ လူအများသည် လက်ရှိလစာကိုပဲ အခြေခံတတ်ကြပါသည်။ ဤသည်မှာ anchoring bias ဖြစ်ပါသည်။ ဦးနှောက်သည် အနီးဆုံး၊ အလွယ်ဆုံးမှတ်မိသောနံပါတ်ကို အခြေခံအဖြစ် ဖမ်းထားပါသည်။ သို့သော် လက်ရှိလစာသည် မိမိ၏ ယနေ့ဈေးကွက်တန်ဖိုး မဟုတ်ပါ။ ၎င်းသည် အတိတ်က negotiation အခြေအနေ၊ အတိတ်က company budget၊ အတိတ်က မိမိမေးရဲမှုတို့၏ ရလဒ်သာ ဖြစ်နိုင်ပါသည်။

ဈေးကွက်တန်ဖိုးကို သိရန် အရင် research လုပ်ပါ။ ReferTRM salary benchmarks၊ JobNet၊ MyJobs၊ work.com.mm စသောနေရာများတွင် တူညီသော job title နှင့် experience level ကို စစ်ပါ။ Basic salary တစ်ခုတည်း မဟုတ်ဘဲ total compensation ကို တွက်ပါ။ Overtime၊ SSB၊ transport allowance၊ meal allowance၊ bonus တို့ကို ထည့်တွက်ပါ။ ဈေးကွက် range ကို သိပြီးမှ expected salary ကို ပြောပါ။

အကောင်းဆုံးနည်းလမ်းမှာ “Reference, Range, Respect” ဖြစ်ပါသည်။ ပထမ Reference - ဈေးကွက် data ကို အခြေခံပါ။ ဒုတိယ Range - တစ်နံပါတ်တည်း မပြောဘဲ range ပြောပါ။ တတိယ Respect - company compensation framework ကို လေးစားကြောင်း ဖော်ပြပါ။ ဤနည်းလမ်းဖြင့် မိမိလိုချင်သည်ကို မတောင်းဆိုသလို မဟုတ်ဘဲ professional preparation ရှိသူအဖြစ် ပြသနိုင်ပါသည်။
