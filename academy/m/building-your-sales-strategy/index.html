Building Your Sales Strategy — WHO, WHAT, HOW

Every successful sale begins long before the pitch. It begins with a strategy — a clear understanding of WHO you are selling to, WHAT you are offering them, and HOW you will reach them. Without this framework, you are throwing darts blindfolded.

WHO: Define your ideal customer with specifics, not generalizations. Instead of "small businesses," say "restaurant owners in Yangon with 10-50 employees who currently manage inventory manually." The more specific your target, the more relevant your approach. In Myanmar, different customer segments respond to very different approaches. A family-owned shop in Mandalay operates differently from a corporate office in Yangon. Know who you serve.

Create a simple customer profile: What is their biggest daily frustration? What have they tried before? What would success look like for them? What is their budget reality? Who else influences their buying decision? In Myanmar business, the decision-maker is often not the person you first meet. Understanding the buying committee — even if it is just the owner and their spouse — saves you months of wasted effort.

WHAT: Your product or service is not what you sell. The outcome is what you sell. A photocopier company does not sell machines — it sells the ability to share documents faster. A recruitment agency does not sell CVs — it sells the confidence that the right person will show up on Monday morning. Translate every feature of your product into a benefit your specific customer cares about.

Build a value matrix: list 5 features of your product. Next to each feature, write the benefit for your target customer. Next to each benefit, write the emotional outcome. People buy on emotion and justify with logic. This is true everywhere, but especially in Myanmar where business relationships carry deep personal trust.

HOW: Choose your channels wisely. In Myanmar, the dominant channels are Viber (personal outreach), Facebook (group selling and ads), phone calls, and in-person visits. Each channel has its own rhythm. Viber messages work best between 9-11am. Facebook posts perform best in the evening. Cold calls convert better after lunch. In-person visits require prior appointment in corporate settings but can be walk-in for small businesses.

Build a weekly activity plan: Monday — research 10 new prospects. Tuesday-Thursday — outreach (calls, messages, visits). Friday — follow up on all pending conversations. Saturday — review metrics and plan next week. Consistency in this cycle will outperform any clever technique.

Real-World Scenario

Ma Thida joined Metro Mart as a field sales rep with zero experience. Her manager gave her a territory map and said "go sell." For three weeks, she walked into random shops and pitched products to anyone who would listen. She sold nothing. Then a senior rep told her: "You are working hard but working blind. Who is your ideal customer? What problem do you solve for them? Until you can answer those two questions, you are just exercising your legs."

## Common Mistake

New sales reps in Myanmar often confuse activity with strategy. Visiting 20 shops a day feels productive, but if those shops do not need your product, you are wasting everyone's time. A sales strategy answers three questions: WHO are my best potential customers? WHAT specific problem do I solve for them? HOW will I reach them efficiently? Without these answers, you are a fisherman casting nets in swimming pools.

## Action Steps

  1. Define your Ideal Customer Profile in one sentence: "[Type of business] in [location] that struggles with [specific problem] and currently uses [current solution or nothing]."
  1. List your top 20 target accounts. Research each one for 10 minutes. Know their business, their pain points, and who makes purchasing decisions.
  1. Create a weekly plan: Monday-Tuesday for new outreach (cold visits), Wednesday-Thursday for follow-ups (warm leads), Friday for pipeline review and admin.

## Reflection Question

Look at your last 10 sales conversations. How many were with people who actually had the budget and authority to buy? If fewer than half, your targeting needs work.
