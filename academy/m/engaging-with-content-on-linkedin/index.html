Engaging with Content on LinkedIn

CORE INSIGHT

LinkedIn's algorithm is simple: it rewards people who participate. Every time you comment on a post, your name and headline appear in front of that person's entire network -- potentially hundreds or thousands of professionals, hiring managers, and recruiters. A thoughtful comment on a Country Director's post can put you in front of more decision-makers than a year of job applications. Most Myanmar professionals treat LinkedIn like a digital CV -- something you set up once and leave. But LinkedIn is not a document. It is a room full of people talking. If you stand in the corner saying nothing, nobody knows you exist.

The key insight is this: commenting is more powerful than posting for beginners. When you post, only your existing network sees it. When you comment on someone else's post -- especially someone with a large following -- their entire audience sees your name, your headline, and your thinking. You are borrowing their stage. This is why a Foundation-level professional with 150 connections can get more visibility from five smart comments per week than from writing original posts that reach 30 people.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

In Myanmar's LinkedIn ecosystem in 2026, the network is still relatively small compared to markets like Singapore or India. This is actually your advantage. When a Country Manager at Lotus Consumer Goods Myanmar or a Regional Director at an established Myanmar bank posts something, they might get 15-30 comments. In Singapore, that same post gets 300. This means your comment in Myanmar is far more likely to be read and remembered. The talent pool that actively engages on LinkedIn in Myanmar is tiny -- perhaps a few thousand people across all industries. By simply showing up consistently, you enter an elite minority.

There is a cultural factor to address directly: many Myanmar professionals feel it is inappropriate or presumptuous to comment on a senior person's post. This hesitation comes from our respect for hierarchy -- we do not want to seem like we are putting ourselves at the same level. But LinkedIn is not a Myanmar office. On this platform, senior professionals expect and welcome engagement. When you comment thoughtfully on a CEO's post, they do not think you are being disrespectful. They think you are engaged, intelligent, and worth remembering. In the NGO and INGO sector especially, hiring managers actively check whether candidates engage with sector content -- it signals genuine interest versus just wanting the salary.

AI tools like ChatGPT can now help you draft comments if you struggle with English phrasing, but the insight must be yours. A recruiter can spot a generic AI comment instantly. Use AI to polish your language, not to replace your thinking.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most common mistake Myanmar professionals make is the empty reaction -- hitting the Like button on 20 posts per day and calling it engagement. Likes are nearly invisible on LinkedIn. The algorithm gives them minimal weight, and nobody remembers who liked their post. The second mistake is writing comments that add nothing: 'Great post!', 'Thanks for sharing!', 'Very insightful!' These are digital noise. Worse, some professionals only engage with content from their own company or their friends, creating an echo chamber that impresses no one outside their existing circle. The consequence is months of activity with zero visibility, zero new connections, and zero recruiter interest -- leading to the false conclusion that LinkedIn does not work in Myanmar.

KEY ACTION STEPS

  1. Identify five LinkedIn accounts to follow this week: two should be senior leaders in your industry in Myanmar (search by company name -- e.g., a major conglomerate, Fresh Market Chain, UNICEF Myanmar), two should be recruiters who post jobs in your field, and one should be a global thought leader in your function. Turn on notifications for all five.
  1. Write one meaningful comment per weekday for the next two weeks. A meaningful comment is 2-4 sentences that adds a perspective, shares a related experience, or asks an intelligent question. Template: 'This resonates because in my experience at [industry/function], I have seen [specific observation]. I am curious whether [thoughtful question].' Spend no more than five minutes per comment.
  1. When you see a Myanmar professional post original content -- especially someone at your level or slightly above -- leave a substantive comment and send them a connection request with a personalized note referencing their post. This builds genuine relationships, not just numbers.
  1. Every Sunday evening, spend 10 minutes reviewing your LinkedIn notifications. Note which comments got replies from the original poster or reactions from others. Double down on the topics and people where engagement is happening.
  1. Set a phone reminder for 8:00 AM Myanmar time. LinkedIn engagement in our timezone peaks between 8-9 AM and 12-1 PM. Commenting during these windows maximizes visibility.
