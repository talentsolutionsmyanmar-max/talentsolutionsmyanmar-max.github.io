Protect Your Job from Day One with a Proper Written Contract

The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Hnin Si worked at a garment factory near Hlaing Tharyar for eleven months. When she started, the supervisor said her salary would be 250,000 kyats per month with overtime pay. Nothing was written down. Ma Hnin Si felt grateful just to have a job and did not want to seem difficult by asking for paper. After eight months, the factory changed ownership. The new manager told her the salary had always been 200,000 kyats. Ma Hnin Si had no evidence. She showed her Kpay transaction history, but the old owner had paid partially in cash. Three coworkers faced the same cut. None of them had written contracts. When Ma Hnin Si went to the Township Labour Office in Hlaing Tharyar, the officer asked for her employment contract. She had nothing. The officer was sympathetic but said without documentation, arbitration would be extremely difficult. Ma Hnin Si lost 50,000 kyats every month for the remaining time she worked there. That is 600,000 kyats per year — gone because of one unsigned piece of paper.

The flipCard 2 of 12
What do you already believe?

Before we begin, answer honestly in your mind: Do you believe that asking your employer for a written contract is disrespectful or shows distrust? Do you believe a verbal promise from a manager is enough to protect your salary and working conditions? Most Myanmar workers believe both of these things — and this belief costs them hundreds of thousands of kyats.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Most Myanmar workers accept verbal job offers without requesting a written contract. The cognitive distortion driving this is Emotional Reasoning: 'I feel grateful to have this job, so asking for a contract would feel ungrateful and therefore must be wrong.' The cultural root cause is face-saving combined with hierarchy respect — questioning a senior person's verbal promise feels like accusing them of dishonesty. The devastating consequence is total vulnerability. When disputes arise about salary, working hours, overtime, or benefits, the worker has zero legal standing. Township Labour Officers cannot mediate effectively without documentation. The worker loses not just one dispute but every future dispute at that workplace, because the employer learns there will never be accountability.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
What Myanmar Labour Law 2013 Actually Requires About Your Contract

Under the Employment and Skill Development Law 2013, every employer in Myanmar is legally required to provide a written employment contract to every worker. This is not optional. This is not a favor. The law specifies the contract must be bilingual — in Myanmar and the worker's language or English if the employer is an international company. The contract must include: job description, salary, working hours, leave entitlements, probation terms, and termination procedures. Here is the perspective shift you need: the law was written to protect you, not your employer. Your employer already knows their rights. The contract exists so that you know yours. Think of the contract as a shield, not a chain. When Nassim Taleb writes about asymmetric risk, this is what he means — spending five minutes reading a contract protects you from months of exploitation. The cost of asking is small discomfort. The cost of not asking is potentially catastrophic. Celebrate the process of requesting and reading the contract. That behavior — regardless of outcome — makes you a more resilient worker.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
How to Get and Use Your Contract in a Myanmar Workplace

In Myanmar's hierarchy-respecting culture, directly demanding a contract from your boss can feel impossible. Here is how to navigate this. First, frame your request as compliance, not confrontation: 'I heard that labour law requires a written contract. I want to make sure our company is following the rules correctly.' This makes you an ally, not an accuser. Second, if your employer refuses, this is itself critical information — a company that refuses a legal requirement is telling you how they will treat you in every future dispute. Document the refusal by saving the date and what was said in your phone notes. Third, at MNC employers like a major multinational based in Yangon, an established Myanmar bank, or a major conglomerate, written contracts are standard during onboarding. If you are interviewing at these companies, you can ask confidently during the offer stage: 'May I review the employment contract before my start date?' This is expected and shows professionalism. Fourth, for local companies where contracts are informal, bring a simple template and suggest it together. Templates are available in Myanmar Labour Law Facebook groups. The key behavior to build: never start working without written terms. Even one week of undocumented work creates a gap that can be exploited.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open your phone's Notes app or Google Keep right now. Type the heading 'My Employment Contract Checklist' and list these 5 items: (1) Job title and description, (2) Salary amount and payment schedule, (3) Working hours and overtime terms, (4) Probation period length, (5) Termination notice requirements. Save this note. Every time you receive a job offer or start a new position, open this checklist and verify all 5 items are written in your contract before signing.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

If you currently have an employment contract, take clear photos of every page right now using your phone camera. Create a folder in Google Drive or your phone's gallery called 'Work Documents.' Store all photos there. If you do not have Google Drive, use the Files app on your Android phone to create the folder. This takes three minutes and protects you if the paper copy is ever lost or destroyed.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Open the Messenger app or Viber. Draft a message template you can send to HR or your supervisor. Write: 'Mingalabar, I would like to respectfully request a copy of my written employment contract for my personal records. I appreciate your help. Thank you.' Save this as a draft. If you do not yet have a contract, modify the message to: 'I would respectfully like to discuss formalizing our employment agreement in writing, as required under the Employment and Skill Development Law.' Do not send it yet — review it after completing this module.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Search Facebook for 'Myanmar Labour Law' or 'Myanmar Workers Rights' groups. Join at least one active group with over 5,000 members. These groups regularly share template contracts, legal updates, and advice from labor advocates. Bookmark the group so you can find it quickly. Do not post personal details publicly — use the group to read and learn from others' experiences with contract disputes.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Download the CamScanner app from the Google Play Store if you have not already. It works on 3G. Practice scanning one document — any document near you — to learn the process. This app turns phone photos into clear PDF scans. From today forward, scan every work-related document you receive: offer letters, pay slips, warning letters, anything. Store all scans in your 'Work Documents' folder. This habit takes 30 seconds per document and builds an unbreakable evidence trail.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

A written contract does not mean you distrust your employer — it means you respect yourself enough to make your agreement real. The worker who asks for a contract is not rude; she is the one the employer cannot cheat later.
