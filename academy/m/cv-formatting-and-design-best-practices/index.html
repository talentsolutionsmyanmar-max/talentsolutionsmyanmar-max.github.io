CORE INSIGHT

The single most important truth about CV design is this: formatting is not decoration -- it is communication infrastructure. A well-formatted CV does not look impressive; it makes your information impossible to miss. The person reading your CV -- whether a Myanmar HR officer screening 200 applications or an ATS (Applicant Tracking System) at an MNC -- spends between 6 and 15 seconds on first scan. In those seconds, your formatting either guides their eyes to your strongest qualifications, or it buries them under visual clutter.

Most Myanmar professionals get this backwards. They believe a more designed CV signals more effort and professionalism. So they add colors, columns, photos with borders, and creative layouts downloaded from Canva or Pinterest. In reality, the most effective CVs at every employer type in Myanmar -- local companies, NGOs, and MNCs alike -- are the plainest ones. One column. Black text on white background. Clear section headings in bold. Consistent spacing. That is it. The companies using ATS software (an established Myanmar bank, a major conglomerate, a mobile payments company, most INGOs, all multinational offices) will literally not see your creative formatting. The software strips it out or, worse, scrambles your information into nonsense. The companies not using ATS are staffed by HR people reviewing stacks of printed CVs or scrolling through email attachments on phones -- and clean formatting wins there too.

THE FORMAT RULES THAT ACTUALLY MATTER

Font: Use Calibri, Arial, or Aptos at 10-12 point size. These render correctly on every device in Myanmar, including older Android phones where many HR staff first open your attachment. Never use Myanmar Unicode fonts for your English CV -- save Myanmar text for a separate Myanmar-language CV if requested.

Margins: 2cm on all sides. This gives your content room to breathe and prints correctly on A4 paper, which is still how many local companies review CVs.

File format: Always PDF. Every time. A Word document reformats itself depending on the device, the operating system version, and which fonts are installed. A PDF looks identical everywhere. Name the file professionally: 'HninEi_AdminCoordinator_CV.pdf' -- not 'My CV final final (2).docx'.

Layout: Single column only. No tables, no text boxes, no graphics, no icons. ATS software cannot read content trapped inside tables or columns. Even for human readers, a single clean column is faster to scan.

Section headings: Bold, slightly larger font (12-14pt for headings, 10-11pt for body). Use the same heading style throughout. The headings act as signposts: Professional Experience, Education, Skills, Languages.

White space: Leave a full line break between sections. Do not cram everything onto one page by removing all spacing. A slightly longer CV that breathes is better than a one-page wall of text. For Foundation-level roles, aim for one page. If you genuinely have more than five years of relevant experience, two pages maximum.

Photo: In Myanmar, most employers expect a passport-style photo on the CV. Use a plain background, professional clothing, and place it in the top-right corner. Do not use a border, filter, or casual photo. If applying to an international MNC that follows Western norms, omit the photo unless the job posting requests one.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

Myanmar's hiring landscape in 2026 is split. Large employers -- banks, telecoms, MNCs, international NGOs -- increasingly use ATS platforms or structured screening in tools like BambooHR, Workday, or even AI-powered screening through platforms like ReferTRM. For these employers, a fancy CV template is not just unnecessary -- it actively sabotages you. The ATS cannot parse columns, and AI screening tools extract text in reading order, so a two-column layout feeds your skills section into the middle of your work history. Meanwhile, many local SMEs and family-owned businesses still review CVs by printing them or opening them on Viber desktop. For them, the question is simply: can I read this clearly in five seconds? In both cases, clean and simple wins. Yangon-based employers receive higher volumes of applications and scan faster. If you are applying outside Yangon -- Mandalay, Taunggyi, Mawlamyine -- the HR person may have even less screening experience and will rely even more heavily on clear formatting to find your relevant details quickly.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most common formatting mistake Myanmar professionals make is using a downloaded creative CV template from Canva, Pinterest, or a Facebook CV group, then submitting it as a Word document. They choose the template because it looks modern and they believe it shows effort. But these templates almost always use two columns, text boxes, tables, skill-level bar charts, and custom fonts -- every single element that breaks in ATS software and on different devices. The consequence: their perfectly good experience becomes unreadable to the system or the person screening. They never get a response and assume they are not qualified, when the reality is their CV was never actually read. This is a formatting failure, not a career failure.
