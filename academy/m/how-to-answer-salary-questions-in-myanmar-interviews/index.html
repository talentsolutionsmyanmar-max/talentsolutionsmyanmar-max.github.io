How to Answer Salary Questions in Myanmar Interviews

CORE INSIGHT

The salary question is not actually about salary. It is a test of three things: whether you know your market value, whether you can communicate with confidence under pressure, and whether your expectations align with what the company has budgeted. Most Myanmar professionals treat it as a trap — something to survive. Professionals who advance treat it as a conversation — something to navigate skillfully.

Here is what most people get wrong: they think the 'safe' answer is to go low. A fresh graduate from Dagon University applies for an admin role at a garment manufacturer. She knows her friends earn 250,000-300,000 MMK. She says '200,000 is fine' because she is afraid the company will reject her. The company had budgeted 350,000 for the role. She gets hired at 220,000 and spends 14 months underpaid — not because the company was unfair, but because she told them a low number and they accepted it. The opposite mistake also kills offers. A candidate for a junior sales role at an FMCG distributor says '600,000' because he heard MNCs pay that range. The company's ceiling is 400,000. There is no second conversation. Both mistakes come from the same root cause: not knowing the actual number before walking in.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

Salary transparency in Myanmar is still low compared to markets like Thailand or Singapore. Most job posts on Myanmar platforms list salary as 'negotiable' or show ranges so wide they are meaningless (200,000-500,000 MMK). This means the research burden falls on you, and most candidates skip it. In local companies, salary is often set by the owner's instinct rather than a formal band structure. In NGOs, salary bands are rigid but published internally — you can ask current staff. In MNCs operating in Myanmar, HR uses regional benchmarking tools and typically will not exceed their band ceiling by more than 5-10%, no matter how much you negotiate. Knowing which type of employer you face changes your entire strategy. One more reality: in Myanmar culture, many candidates feel that discussing money directly is disrespectful to the interviewer. This is a misunderstanding. The interviewer asked you the question. They expect a direct answer. Respectful does not mean vague.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most damaging mistake Myanmar professionals make is saying 'I will accept whatever you offer' or in Myanmar, 'ဘာပဲပေးပေး လက်ခံပါတယ်'. Candidates say this because they believe it shows flexibility and humility — traits valued in Myanmar culture. What it actually communicates to the interviewer is: this person has not researched their value, does not know what the role pays, and may accept an offer but resent it within three months. HR managers at mid-size Yangon companies have told us directly: when a candidate says this, it lowers their perceived professionalism immediately.

THE ACTION

  1. Before any interview, open ReferTRM, JobNet, and MyJobs. Search your exact job title filtered by Yangon. Note the salary range shown in at least 5 current listings. Write down the lowest, highest, and middle number.
  2. Ask one person who works in a similar role at a similar company type what they earn. In Myanmar, the indirect way works: 'I have an interview for a junior accountant role at a local trading company — what range do you think is normal?' Most people will answer this version.
  3. Decide your three numbers before the interview: your ideal salary, your realistic salary, and your walk-away minimum. Write all three on a piece of paper and put it in your pocket.
  4. Practice this sentence out loud three times: 'Based on my research and the responsibilities of this role, I am looking at a range of [realistic number] to [ideal number]. I am open to discussing this based on the full package including any benefits.'
  5. If the interviewer asks for your current salary instead of your expected salary, you can answer honestly but always add: 'My expectation for this role is [number] based on the expanded responsibilities.'

DECISION SCENARIO

Ma Hnin Ei is interviewing for a Customer Service Supervisor role at a logistics company in Hlaing Tharyar. She currently earns 320,000 MMK as a senior customer service agent at a local company. She has researched and found that supervisor roles at similar companies pay 400,000-500,000 MMK. The interviewer says: 'What is your expected salary?' Which response serves Ma Hnin Ei best?

A) 'I currently earn 320,000, so anything above that would be fine.'
B) 'Based on my research into supervisor roles in this industry, I am looking at a range of 420,000 to 480,000 MMK, and I am open to discussing this based on the full compensation package.'
C) 'I would prefer not to discuss salary until I receive an offer.'
D) 'I expect 550,000 MMK because I have three years of experience and supervisors should earn more.'
