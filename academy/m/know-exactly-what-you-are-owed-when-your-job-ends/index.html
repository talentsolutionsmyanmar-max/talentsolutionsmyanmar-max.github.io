The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Ei Ei Mon was a sales coordinator at an electronics shop near Yuzana Plaza for four years. One Monday morning, the owner called her in and said business was bad and her position was eliminated. He told her to collect her things and leave by noon. He handed her an envelope with her final month's salary and said 'thank you for your hard work.' Ma Ei Ei Mon cried on the bus home. She had 150,000 kyats in her a mobile payments company account and a son starting Grade 5. She posted her story in a Facebook jobs group, and within an hour, multiple people told her she was entitled to termination notice or pay in lieu of notice, and potentially compensation for her four years of service. She had been owed at minimum one month's notice — or one month's salary as compensation for no notice. The shop owner knew this. He had offered her nothing beyond what he already owed for her final working month. Ma Ei Ei Mon went back three days later with information from a labour rights volunteer. The owner, confronted with specifics, paid her the notice period compensation. But Ma Ei Ei Mon knew she had almost walked away from money that was legally hers.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Myanmar workers who are terminated accept whatever the employer gives in that moment and leave without questioning whether it meets legal requirements, often due to shock and the desire to preserve dignity. The cognitive distortion is Catastrophizing combined with Emotional Reasoning: 'I just got fired, everything is falling apart, I need to get out of here before I lose face further.' The cultural root cause is face-saving at its most destructive — being terminated already causes deep shame in Myanmar's loyalty-based work culture, and the last thing a worker wants to do is appear to be begging or fighting. The devastating consequence is workers forfeiting weeks or months of compensation, unpaid leave balances, outstanding SSB contributions, and notice period payments — often the equivalent of one to three months of income that their families desperately need during the transition to new employment.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
What Myanmar Law Requires When Your Employment Ends

When an employer terminates your employment in Myanmar, several legal obligations activate regardless of the reason for termination. First, Notice Period: employers must provide advance notice of termination. For workers employed longer than 6 months, this is typically one month. If the employer wants you to leave immediately, they must pay you one month's salary in lieu of notice. This is not generosity — it is law. Second, Outstanding Wages: every kyat you have earned through your last working day must be paid, including any overtime. Third, Unused Leave Compensation: if you have accumulated annual leave days that you did not use, these must be paid out at your daily wage rate. Fourth, SSB Record: the employer must provide documentation of your SSB contributions so you can transfer or access your benefits. Fifth, Service Certificate: you are entitled to a letter confirming your employment dates and position, which you need for future job applications. Here is the asymmetric thinking principle that protects you: the small cost of asking for these items is momentary discomfort. The massive cost of not asking is losing months of income you legally earned. Your employer's urgency to have you leave quickly is itself a signal that they know they owe you more than they are offering.

How to Protect Yourself Before and During Termination in Myanmar

The most important termination preparation happens while you are still employed. Build your evidence archive monthly — contracts, pay slips, SSB records, performance reviews, important messages. Companies like an established Myanmar bank and a major multinational based in Yangon provide digital pay slips and formal termination processes, but many Myanmar employers operate informally, which means the burden of documentation falls on you. When termination happens, your immediate reaction should be this phrase: 'I understand. May I have the termination in writing and a breakdown of my final payment? I will review everything within 48 hours.' This single sentence transforms the dynamic. It signals that you are calm, informed, and will not be rushed. It gives you time to check your rights document, calculate what you are owed, and consult a labor rights group on Facebook or visit the Township Labour Office. Never sign a separation agreement, clearance form, or any document on the spot. The pressure to sign immediately is a tactic — not a requirement. Myanmar's face-saving culture makes this difficult because you may feel that requesting time looks confrontational. Reframe it: requesting time to review is what professional, competent workers do. It is the behavior of someone MNC employers would hire. Even if you feel devastated, following your prepared script keeps you from making decisions you cannot reverse.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open your phone notes and create a document titled 'If I Am Terminated — My Rights.' Write the following: (1) I am owed all wages through my last working day, (2) I am owed payment for any unused annual leave, (3) I am owed notice period compensation if terminated without proper notice — minimum one month for workers with more than 6 months service, (4) I must receive my SSB contribution record, (5) I must receive a service certificate. Save this document. Review it now while you are calm — you will not be able to think clearly in the moment of termination.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

Calculate your current 'termination value' right now. Open your calculator. Add: (A) your current month's salary owed to date, (B) your unused annual leave days multiplied by your daily wage, (C) one month's salary as notice period compensation. Write this total in your notes. Update it every month. Knowing this number means you will never accept less than what is legally owed. For example, if your salary is 300,000 kyats and you have 8 unused leave days, your minimum termination value is likely over 400,000 kyats beyond your final paycheck.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Take photos of every document related to your current employment and save them in your Work Documents folder right now: your contract, offer letter, any salary adjustment letters, SSB card, pay slips if you have them, and any performance reviews or commendation messages on Viber or Messenger. Screenshot Viber conversations where your salary or position was discussed. Once you are terminated, you may lose access to company systems and documents immediately. Building this archive while employed is the single most valuable preparation you can make.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Search Facebook for 'Myanmar labour office' or 'Township Labour Office complaint' and read at least 3 posts from workers who filed complaints after unfair termination. Note the process: most went to the Township Labour Office, filed a complaint, and the office called both parties for mediation. Understanding this process while calm eliminates the paralysis you would feel trying to figure it out during a crisis. Save the address and any contact information for the Township Labour Office nearest your workplace.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Write an emergency response script in your notes that you will follow if terminated. Write: 'Step 1: Stay calm and listen, do not sign anything immediately. Step 2: Ask for written termination notice stating the reason and effective date. Step 3: Ask for a breakdown of all final payments including notice compensation and unused leave. Step 4: Say I need 48 hours to review before signing any separation documents. Step 5: Go home and review my termination rights document. Step 6: If payment is below legal requirements, contact Township Labour Office within one week.' Having this script prepared means your future self has a plan even when your emotions are overwhelmed.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

The moment you are most emotionally devastated — being fired — is the exact moment you need legal knowledge most. Employers who terminate you are counting on your shock and shame to prevent you from claiming what the law requires them to pay.
