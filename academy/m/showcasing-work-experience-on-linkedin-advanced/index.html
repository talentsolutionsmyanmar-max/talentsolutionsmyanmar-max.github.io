CORE INSIGHT

The experience section on LinkedIn is not your CV pasted online. It is a searchable, scannable story that must answer one question for every recruiter who lands on it: 'Can this person deliver results in a role like the one I am hiring for?' Most Myanmar professionals treat this section as a duty list -- 'Managed daily operations. Coordinated with stakeholders. Prepared reports.' These phrases say you had a job. They do not say you were good at it.

What separates a profile that gets recruiter messages from one that does not is specificity. Instead of 'Managed daily operations,' write 'Managed warehouse logistics for 120 SKUs across 3 Yangon distribution points, reducing delivery delays by 30% in 6 months.' The second version contains keywords a recruiter would actually search for, a scale that shows your level, and a result that proves competence. In 2026, many recruiters -- and increasingly AI sourcing tools -- scan for exactly these patterns. If your experience reads like a generic job description, algorithms skip you before a human ever sees your name.

The contrast is stark. A finance officer at a Mandalay construction firm who writes 'Handled accounts payable and receivable for 15 active project sites, processing 200+ invoices monthly with zero audit exceptions over 2 years' will outperform someone who writes 'Responsible for financial tasks' -- even if the second person is more skilled. LinkedIn rewards those who articulate, not just those who perform.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

In Myanmar, three dynamics shape how your experience section works. First, many local companies do not have well-known names. If you worked at 'Golden Star Co., Ltd.' a recruiter outside Myanmar -- or even in a different industry within Yangon -- has no context. You must add a brief company description: 'Golden Star Co., Ltd. -- a 200-employee FMCG distributor covering Upper Myanmar.' This one line transforms your credibility. Second, NGO and INGO roles often use internal jargon -- 'Livelihood Officer,' 'WASH Coordinator' -- that MNC recruiters do not search for. If you want to move from the development sector into the private sector, translate your titles into commercial language in the description, even if you keep the official title. Third, AI-powered recruiter tools on LinkedIn in 2026 index your descriptions for skill keywords. If your experience section does not contain the specific skills from job postings you want -- 'supply chain,' 'stakeholder management,' 'budget forecasting' -- you are invisible to these tools regardless of your actual ability.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most common mistake Myanmar professionals make is copying their job description from their appointment letter directly into LinkedIn. This happens because many people were never taught the difference between responsibilities and achievements, and because modesty -- deeply valued in Myanmar culture -- makes people uncomfortable claiming credit for results. The consequence is a profile full of generic duties that no recruiter can distinguish from anyone else's. Your appointment letter describes the role. Your LinkedIn should describe what you specifically made happen in that role.

ACTION STEPS

  1. Open your LinkedIn profile right now. For your current role, delete any sentence that starts with 'Responsible for' and rewrite it using this formula: Action verb + what you did + scale or scope + result or impact. Example: 'Trained 25 new sales staff across 4 branches, contributing to a 15% increase in Q3 regional revenue.'
  1. For each role, add a one-line company description if the company is not internationally recognized. Format: 'Company Name -- [employee count]-employee [industry] company [geographic scope].'
  1. Search LinkedIn for 3 job postings you would want next. List the top 10 skill keywords that appear repeatedly. Check whether those exact words appear anywhere in your experience descriptions. Add the ones that honestly reflect your work.
  1. For roles where you cannot quantify results with numbers, use scope instead: team size managed, number of clients served, geographic area covered, project budget handled.
  1. Ask a trusted colleague: 'What is the most useful thing I did in my role here?' Their answer often reveals achievements you have normalized and forgotten to mention.

DECISION SCENARIO

Ko Zaw has been a Marketing Executive at a mid-size Yangon real estate company for 18 months. He managed social media, organized 4 property launch events, and grew the company Facebook page from 8,000 to 35,000 followers. He is updating his LinkedIn. Which description is most effective?

A) 'Marketing Executive responsible for social media management and event organization.'
B) 'Managed digital marketing campaigns and grew company Facebook following from 8,000 to 35,000 in 18 months. Planned and executed 4 property launch events averaging 200+ attendees each, generating 50+ qualified leads per event.'
C) 'Handled all marketing duties including social media, events, content creation, branding, PR, and stakeholder engagement.'
D) 'Creative and passionate marketing professional with experience in real estate sector social media and events.'

KEY TAKEAWAY

Your LinkedIn experience section is not a record of where you worked -- it is evidence of what you are capable of. Every line should make a recruiter think 'this person delivers results,' not 'this person had a job.'
