The storyCard 1 of 12
The Story

Ma Thida works in customer service at a microfinance company near Sule Pagoda in downtown Yangon. She was offered a team leader position at a competing company in Kamayut township. The new company offered 500,000 kyats per month — the same as her current salary. Ma Thida almost rejected the offer immediately. Same salary, why move? But her friend who works in HR at a mobile payments company told her to ask about the full package. Ma Thida asked. The new role included company-provided SIM with unlimited data, transportation allowance of 50,000 kyats, annual bonus of two months salary, SSB coverage at 3% employer contribution, and five extra annual leave days. When Ma Thida calculated everything, the total compensation was equivalent to over 620,000 kyats per month. She had almost rejected an offer worth 120,000 kyats more per month because she only looked at one number. In Myanmar, where power outages and transport costs eat into wages daily, the benefits around the salary often matter more than the salary itself.

The flipCard 2 of 12
What do you already believe?

If two companies offer you the same job and one pays 500,000 kyats monthly and the other pays 480,000 kyats monthly, which offer is better? Most people answer instantly. But what if you do not know the bonus structure, leave policy, or allowances of either company? On what basis are you actually deciding?

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

The biggest mistake is tunnel vision on the monthly salary number while ignoring transportation allowance, phone allowance, overtime policy, annual bonus structure, SSB contributions, annual leave, training budget, and promotion timeline. The cognitive distortion is narrow framing — evaluating a complex multi-variable offer through a single variable. The cultural root cause is that Myanmar workers are conditioned to discuss salary as the only compensation metric because informal employment dominated for decades and benefits were rare. The devastating consequence is accepting lower total compensation while believing you negotiated well, or rejecting genuinely superior offers because the base salary appeared unchanged. Over a career, missed benefits can represent millions of kyats in lost value.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
The hidden wealth inside your employment contract that most workers never calculate

Myanmar Labor Law 2013 requires employers to provide certain benefits including SSB contributions (employer pays 3%, employee pays 2%), written contracts in a language the worker understands, and maximum three-month probation periods. Beyond legal minimums, many employers in Myanmar now offer transportation allowances (critical when Yangon fuel prices fluctuate), phone or data allowances, annual bonuses ranging from one to three months salary, and training budgets. Here is the math that changes everything: a job paying 450,000 kyats base salary with 50,000 transport allowance, two-month annual bonus, and 30,000 data allowance is worth approximately 555,000 kyats per month in total compensation. A competing offer of 500,000 kyats base with no benefits is worth exactly 500,000. The lower base salary job is actually worth 55,000 more per month. MJ DeMarco teaches that financial intelligence means seeing the full picture, not the most visible number. Every time you evaluate only salary, you are making a decision with incomplete information. The process is: list every component, assign a value, then compare totals.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
How to ask about benefits in Myanmar without appearing greedy or presumptuous

In Myanmar's hierarchical culture, asking detailed questions about benefits during an offer can feel like you are being demanding. The solution is framing and timing. First, timing: ask about benefits after you receive a formal offer, never during the first interview. This shows you are evaluating seriously, not shopping. Second, framing: use curiosity language, not demand language. Say 'I would love to understand the full compensation structure so I can make a thoughtful decision' rather than 'What else do you give me?' Third, normalize it by referencing standard practice: 'I understand many companies in this industry provide transportation support — could you share how that works here?' At companies like an established Myanmar bank, a major conglomerate, and international employers, HR departments have structured benefits packages ready to explain. At smaller local companies, benefits may be informal and negotiable — which actually means more flexibility for you if you know to ask. The worst that happens when you ask about benefits is that you learn there are none. The best is that you discover thousands of kyats in value you would have otherwise missed.

Do thisCard 6 of 12
Action 1

Open the Notes app on your phone and create a document titled 'Total Compensation Checklist.' Write these categories as a list: Base Salary, Transportation Allowance, Phone or Data Allowance, Meal Allowance, Annual Bonus (number of months), SSB Employer Contribution, Annual Leave Days, Sick Leave Days, Overtime Pay Policy, Training or Education Budget, Insurance Coverage, Promotion Timeline, Work From Home Days, Uniform or Equipment Provided. Next to each item, write what your current employer provides. For items you do not know, put a question mark. This becomes your baseline for any future offer comparison.

Do thisCard 7 of 12
Action 2

Open your phone calculator. Take your current annual leave days and multiply by your daily salary (monthly salary divided by 22 working days). This is the monetary value of your leave benefit. Do the same for any transportation allowance (multiply monthly amount by 12). Add your annual bonus if any. Add your SSB employer contribution (3% of your salary times 12 months per Myanmar Labor Law 2013). Write down the total annual value of all your non-salary benefits. Most Myanmar workers are shocked to discover benefits represent 15-30% of their total compensation. You cannot negotiate what you cannot measure.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Open MyJobs.com.mm or work.com.mm on your phone. Find three job postings in your field that list benefits. Screenshot each one and save to your Market Value folder. Compare their benefits sections side by side. Notice which companies offer transportation allowance, which offer bonuses, and which mention SSB. This research shows you what is normal to request in your industry — you are not inventing demands, you are asking for what other employers already provide as standard.

Do thisCard 9 of 12
Action 4

Draft a message in your Notes app with three benefit questions you will ask at your next offer discussion. Use this format: 'Could you help me understand the full compensation structure? Specifically, I would appreciate learning about [1] the annual bonus structure and typical payout, [2] transportation or commuting support, and [3] professional development or training opportunities.' These questions are professional, expected by HR at any serious company, and signal that you evaluate opportunities like an experienced professional, not a desperate applicant.

Do thisCard 10 of 12
Action 5

Create a simple comparison spreadsheet in Google Sheets on your phone (free app, works on 3G). Make two columns: Current Job and New Offer. List every compensation element from your checklist. Fill in monetary values for each. Calculate the total annual compensation for each column. Save this template — you will use it for every future job offer for the rest of your career. Having a visual comparison removes emotion from the decision and reveals the true math that salaries alone cannot show.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

The monthly salary number is the most visible part of compensation and the least important part of wealth building. Workers who negotiate only salary leave the biggest money on the table in benefits they never asked about.
