Myanmar is a market where relationships move through specific channels, and mastering each channel is as important as mastering your sales pitch. The four primary sales communication channels each serve different purposes at different stages of the customer journey.

Viber is Myanmar's business communication backbone. Over 90% of business conversations happen here, from initial inquiries to order confirmations. Best practices: keep messages under 3 lines for initial outreach. Include your name and company in every first message. Use voice messages for complex explanations — they feel more personal than text and take less time for both parties. Never send bulk promotional messages without permission. Send follow-ups between 9-11am or 2-4pm when people are most responsive. Always reply within 2 hours during business hours. If you cannot solve their request immediately, acknowledge it and give a specific time you will follow up.

Facebook serves as both your storefront and your community. For B2C sales, Facebook Page and Marketplace are essential. For B2B, Facebook Groups in your industry connect you with decision-makers who are actively discussing relevant problems. Post valuable content 3-4 times per week. Respond to every comment, even simple ones — the algorithm rewards engagement and people notice responsiveness. Facebook Ads can be highly effective with budgets as low as 5,000 MMK per day when targeted correctly to location, age, and interests.

Phone calls remain the fastest way to move a deal forward. In Myanmar business culture, a phone call signals seriousness. Use calls for: confirming meetings, discussing pricing, handling objections that require nuance, and closing deals. Before calling, prepare 3 talking points. Start every call with a warm reference — "I am following up on our Viber conversation about..." Keep calls focused and under 10 minutes unless the prospect wants to talk longer. Always end with a specific next step: "I will send the quotation by 3pm today."

In-person visits are the highest-trust channel. For large deals, corporate accounts, and relationship-critical moments, nothing replaces showing up. In Myanmar culture, face-to-face meetings carry implicit respect. Dress appropriately — business casual for most industries, formal for banking and corporate. Bring printed materials. Arrive 5 minutes early. Accept tea or water when offered — declining can be interpreted as disinterest. During the meeting, listen more than you speak. Take notes visibly — it shows you value their words.

Channel strategy: Use Facebook to generate awareness, Viber for nurturing relationships, phone calls for advancing deals, and in-person visits for closing. Move prospects through channels as trust deepens, never skipping stages.

Real-World Scenario

Ko Htun Htun only used phone calls to reach clients. His close rate was 8%. When he added Viber messaging to his process — sending a brief intro message before calling — his close rate jumped to 22%. Why? In Myanmar, an unexpected phone call from an unknown number feels intrusive. A Viber message gives the recipient time to read, think, and decide whether to engage. The channel you choose is as important as the message you send.

## Common Mistake

Using the wrong communication channel for the wrong stage kills deals. Cold outreach via phone call feels aggressive. Follow-up via email gets lost (many Myanmar professionals check email once a day). Sending a proposal via Viber message feels unprofessional. Match the channel to the purpose: Viber for initial contact and quick follow-ups, phone for scheduled conversations, email for formal proposals and documentation, in-person for negotiations and closing.

## Action Steps

  1. Map your sales process to channels: Initial contact → Viber message. Follow-up → Viber voice note. Product demo → In-person or video call. Proposal → Email with PDF. Negotiation → In-person meeting.
  1. Create templates for each channel. A Viber intro template, a follow-up template, a proposal email template. Templates save time and ensure consistency.
  1. Track which channel gets the best response rate for YOUR specific customers. B2B clients in Yangon may prefer email; wholesale buyers in Mandalay may prefer Viber. Let data guide you, not assumptions.

## Reflection Question

Think about how YOU prefer to be contacted by salespeople. Phone call? Viber? Email? Your customers probably have similar preferences. Ask your next 5 clients: "What is the best way to reach you?" and update your approach.
