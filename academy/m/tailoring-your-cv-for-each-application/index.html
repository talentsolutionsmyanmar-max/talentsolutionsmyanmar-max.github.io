CORE INSIGHT

The single idea that changes everything about CV writing: your CV is not a record of your history -- it is an argument for why you are the answer to a specific employer's problem. Most Myanmar professionals treat their CV like a biography. They list every duty from every job in chronological order and send the same document everywhere. But hiring managers do not read CVs start to finish. They scan for 6-10 seconds, looking for proof that you can do the specific job they need to fill. If your most relevant experience is buried in the third bullet point of your second job listing, they will never see it.

Here is the contrast. A generic CV for a Sales Coordinator role might say: 'Responsible for daily office administration, coordinating with suppliers, and supporting the sales team.' A tailored CV for the same person applying to a Sales Coordinator role at a distribution company says: 'Coordinated sales pipeline tracking for 15 SKUs across 3 regions, supporting a team that exceeded quarterly targets by 12%.' Same experience. Completely different impact. The tailored version mirrors the language and priorities from the job posting.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

In Myanmar, tailoring matters even more than in global markets -- but for reasons most job seekers do not realize. First, many local companies (especially in FMCG, construction, and banking) use manual screening, not ATS software. That means a real human -- often a junior HR assistant earning 300,000-400,000 MMK -- is scanning your CV with a checklist from the hiring manager. If the job posting says 'experience with distributor management' and your CV says 'channel operations,' that HR assistant may not make the connection. You must use their exact words. Second, at NGOs and INGOs like Save the Children or UNDP, the screening is often done against a competency matrix. If the posting lists 'monitoring and evaluation experience,' you need that precise phrase on your CV, not 'data analysis' or 'reporting.' Third, MNCs operating in Myanmar increasingly use ATS platforms like Workday or SAP SuccessFactors. These systems filter by keyword match before any human sees your application. If you do not include the right terms, your CV is rejected by software, not a person.

The rise of AI tools in 2026 adds a new dimension. Many candidates now use ChatGPT to generate CVs. Hiring managers know this. A clearly tailored CV -- one that references the specific company's product, market, or challenge -- now signals genuine effort and stands out even more against a flood of AI-generated generic applications.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most common mistake Myanmar job seekers make is creating one 'perfect' CV and sending it unchanged to every job. The cultural root is understandable -- in Myanmar education, we learn there is one correct answer, one correct format. Many professionals also believe that changing your CV for each job is somehow dishonest, as if you are pretending to be different people. The truth is the opposite. Tailoring is not lying -- it is relevance. You are choosing which true parts of your experience to emphasize. The consequence of not tailoring is brutal: you apply to 50 jobs and hear nothing, then conclude the job market is impossible, when the real problem was that you sent a construction company the same CV you sent a bank.

THE ACTION

  1. Create a master CV document that lists every role, every achievement, every skill you have ever developed. This is your personal archive -- it will never be sent to anyone. Save it as 'Master CV - [Your Name]' on your phone or Google Drive.
  1. For your next application, open the job posting and highlight or write down the 5 most important requirements. Look at the 'Responsibilities' and 'Requirements' sections specifically. Write these 5 items on a piece of paper or in your phone notes.
  1. Open your master CV and find experiences that match those 5 requirements. Copy only the relevant bullet points into a new document. Rewrite each bullet point using the exact language from the job posting. If they say 'stakeholder engagement,' do not write 'working with partners.'
  1. Rewrite your CV summary (the 2-3 sentence section at the top) to directly address the role. Include the job title, the industry, and one specific achievement. Example: 'Sales Coordinator with 3 years of experience in FMCG distribution across Yangon and Mandalay, with a track record of supporting teams that consistently exceeded quarterly sales targets.'
  1. Before submitting, do a 30-second test: read only the top half of your CV. Does it clearly answer the question 'Can this person do the specific job we posted?' If not, reorder your bullet points so the most relevant ones appear first.

DECISION SCENARIO

Ma Aye Aye sees a job posting for an Administrative Officer at an INGO in Yangon. The posting emphasizes 'procurement experience, donor reporting, and fluency in English.' Ma Aye Aye has done procurement at her current local company, wrote reports for her manager (not donors), and speaks English well. Her current CV leads with 'office management and filing' because that was her first duty at her first job. She has 30 minutes before the deadline. What should she do?

Option A: Submit her current CV as-is because changing it last minute might introduce errors.
Option B: Rewrite her CV summary to mention procurement and reporting, and move her procurement bullet points to the top of her experience section.
Option C: Remove all non-procurement experience from her CV so it looks completely focused.
Option D: Copy phrases from the job posting directly into her CV summary without connecting them to her actual experience.
