The storyCard 1 of 8
The Story

Same req, two recruiters. Ko Paing sends eighteen CVs in three days. Two get accepted for interview; by Friday U Kyaw Zin stops opening his emails. Ma Thiri, after her intake and her hunt, sends four submittals in five days. Three are accepted; two reach interview. Do the arithmetic the hiring manager's thumb does unconsciously: Ko Paing's emails are right about one time in nine; Ma Thiri's are right three times in four. Whose next email gets opened? Now the part nobody teaches: the same arithmetic decides Ma Thiri's income. When her candidate is hired at 1,000,000 MMK a month, the client pays ReferTRM a fee of 700,000 MMK — and Ma Thiri, as the sourcer, takes half of it: 350,000 MMK from one placement, more than many monthly salaries. Close three in a month and she has matched a manager's pay — from a phone and a search string. But only accepted, interviewed, HIRED-and-STAYED candidates reach that ledger. Eighteen ignored CVs pay exactly zero.

The teachingCard 2 of 8
The Recruiter's Scorecard — five numbers
  1. SUBMIT-TO-ACCEPT — of your submittals, how many the client agrees to interview. THE quality number (from M1). A sniper lives at 60–80%; a spammer at 10%. 2) TIME TO FIRST SUBMITTAL (TTFS) — how fast your first QUALIFIED candidate lands after a thorough intake. Around two days is strong. Speed counts only after calibration — a fast wrong submittal is minus, not plus. 3) INTERVIEW-TO-OFFER — of your interviewed candidates, how many get offers. This number grades your SCREENING (M4): if interviews keep dying, your screen is letting the wrong stories through. 4) OFFER-ACCEPTANCE — of the offers made, how many candidates say yes. This grades your expectation-setting: salary honesty, commute reality, no surprises at the end. 5) VERIFIED CLOSES — hires that were accepted AND survived probation, confirmed on the server. This is the number that moves your commission ladder — and the only one nobody can inflate, because the system, not the recruiter, counts it. THE ANTI-METRIC: raw submittal volume. It correlates with being ignored.
The teachingCard 3 of 8
The money, exactly

ReferTRM's model, plainly: the CLIENT pays the fee — 70% of the hired candidate's first-month salary. The candidate never pays anything; a worker never pays to get work. As the sourcer you start at 50% of that fee. Close three verified placements and your cut rises to 60%; close six, 70%. The ladder runs on VERIFIED closes only — server-confirmed, after the hire sticks — so quality literally compounds into rate. Payment follows the fee's collection (or hire with a probation clawback), which is why the next block matters more than any closing trick.

The teachingCard 4 of 8
Verified means it lasted

A close is verified when the candidate accepted AND stayed through probation. Push a doubtful candidate into a job to hit your month-end, and probation gives the truth back: they leave, the fee claws back, the close un-counts, and both the client's and the candidate's trust leave with it. You paid twice for one bad push. The professional habit: surface doubts BEFORE the offer — don't sell, help decide. A candidate who chose with open eyes stays; a candidate who was pressured becomes your clawback. On the scorecard, patience is not softness; it is compounding.

The teachingCard 5 of 8
Numbers with face

Reporting numbers upward is delicate: a stalled search can read as your failure, and saying "the salary is too low" to a senior client can read as challenge. Do it with the M1 reframe and the data: "Saya, here's what the search found — twelve strong profiles, but at this band they're all at 1.2M or above. What I'm finding is incongruent with our assumptions — shall we adjust the band or trade one must-have?" Numbers let you disagree respectfully: it is the MARKET saying it, not you. Never inflate numbers to save face — one discovered inflation costs every future report its credibility.

The common mistakeCard 6 of 8

Chasing volume when the ratio drops. When acceptance falls, the amateur sends MORE; the professional stops and re-runs the intake ("what changed? which assumption broke?"). Close cousins: counting offers as closes before probation; hiding a wobbling candidate's doubts from the client; quoting salary bands you never confirmed.

Do thisCard 7 of 8
  1. Compute your current submit-to-accept from your own submittals (even if the sample is tiny). 2) Set your two working targets: ≥60% accept, TTFS ≤3 days after intake. 3) On your live req, write the one-line money math: salary × 70% × your current tier — see what THIS search pays. 4) List any wobble (commute, salary, shift) your current shortlist carries and decide what you'll surface to whom BEFORE any offer. Verified means it lasted

Verified ဆိုတာ Candidate အလုပ်ဝင်သွားတာနဲ့တင်မပြီးဘဲ တည်မြဲသွားတာကိုဆိုလိုပါတယ်။ Close တစ်ခု Verified Close ဖြစ်ဖို့ Candidate က Offer လက်ခံရမယ်။ Probation ကာလကိုလည်း ကျော်ရမယ်။ လကုန်ခါနီး Close Count လိုချင်လို့ စိတ်မသေချာသေးတဲ့ Candidate တစ်ယောက်ကို အတင်းလက်ခံခိုင်းလိုက်ရင် Probation ရောက်တဲ့အခါ အမှန်ပေါ်လာပါတယ်။ Candidate ကအလုပ်ထွက်သွားနိုင်ပါတယ်။ Client Fee ကိုပြန်လျှော့ရတာ ဒါမှမဟုတ် ပြန်ပေးရတာဖြစ်နိုင်ပါတယ်။ Close Count လည်းပြန်လျော့သွားနိုင်ပါတယ်။ Client ရဲ့ယုံကြည်မှုထိခိုက်သလို Candidate ရဲ့ယုံကြည်မှုလည်း ထိခိုက်ပါတယ်။ Candidate တစ်ယောက်ကို မသေချာဘဲဖိအားပေးထားတဲ့ Hire တစ်ခုကြောင့် Recruiter က နှစ်ဖက်စလုံးမှာ နစ်နာရပါတယ်။ Professional Recruiter တစ်ယောက်လုပ်သင့်တာက Candidate ရဲ့စိတ်ပူနေတဲ့အချက်တွေကို Offer မထွက်ခင်ကတည်းက ရှင်းထားဖို့ပါ။ အလုပ်ကိုရောင်းသလို အတင်းမပြောပါနဲ့။ Candidate ကို အချက်အလက်ပြည့်ပြည့်စုံစုံနဲ့ ကိုယ်တိုင်ဆုံးဖြတ်နိုင်အောင်ကူညီပါ။ အခြေအနေအားလုံးသိပြီးမှ လက်ခံတဲ့ Candidate က အလုပ်ထဲမှာတည်မြဲနိုင်ချေပိုများပါတယ်။ ဖိအားကြောင့် Yes ပြောထားတဲ့ Candidate က Clawback ဖြစ်နိုင်ချေပိုများပါတယ်။
