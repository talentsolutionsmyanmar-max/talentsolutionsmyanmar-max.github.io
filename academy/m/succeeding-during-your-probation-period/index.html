Succeeding During Your Probation Period

CORE INSIGHT

Probation is not a waiting period. It is an audition where the criteria are mostly unwritten. Your manager formed a first impression of you within 72 hours and is now looking for evidence that confirms or contradicts it. The professionals who pass probation consistently are not the ones who work the hardest -- they are the ones who figure out what 'good' looks like in their specific team and then visibly deliver it. Most Myanmar professionals treat probation as 'don't make mistakes and be polite.' That is the minimum to not get fired. To actually succeed -- to get confirmed early, to be first in line for a raise, to become someone the team protects -- you need a different approach: make your manager's life easier in ways they did not expect.

The difference is between passive survival and active proof. Passive survival means showing up on time, saying yes to everything, staying quiet in meetings. Active proof means sending your manager a short update every Friday ('This week I completed X, Y, Z. Next week I plan to do A, B. Any adjustments?'). That single habit changes how your manager perceives you because in most Myanmar offices, nobody does this voluntarily.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

In local Myanmar companies, probation rules are often vague. Many employers extend probation to six months without formal notification. Some use probation as a way to pay a lower salary indefinitely -- if your offer letter says 'salary during probation: 280,000 MMK, confirmed salary: 350,000 MMK,' you need to know your confirmation date precisely. In NGOs and MNCs, probation tends to be more structured with formal review meetings, but even there, the real evaluation happens in hallway conversations between your manager and HR. Gender matters here: women in Myanmar offices often receive less direct feedback during probation because managers avoid what they perceive as confrontation. If you are not hearing anything, it does not mean you are doing well -- it may mean no one wants to tell you the truth.

AI changes this in 2026: some larger employers now use onboarding platforms that track task completion during probation. If your company uses one, treat every logged task as evidence of your contribution. If they do not, create your own tracking -- a simple notebook or phone note with dates and accomplishments.

COMMON MISTAKE

The most damaging mistake Myanmar professionals make during probation is avoiding their manager. Culturally, many new employees feel that approaching their boss too often shows weakness or incompetence. So they guess instead of asking, stay silent instead of clarifying, and wait for their manager to come to them. The consequence is predictable: misaligned work, repeated corrections, and a manager who concludes 'this person cannot work independently.' The irony is that asking one clear question on Monday morning prevents three mistakes by Friday.

ACTION STEPS

  1. On your first day or this week, ask your manager directly: 'What would make you confident about confirming me at the end of probation? What are the two or three things you most want to see?' Write down the answer word for word.
  1. Every Friday, send your manager a message -- Viber, email, or in person -- summarizing what you completed that week and what you plan for next week. Keep it under five sentences. Do this every single week of probation without exception.
  1. Find the one person on your team who has been there longest. Buy them tea. Ask them: 'What do new people usually get wrong here?' Their answer is worth more than the employee handbook.
  1. Start a probation journal on your phone. Every day, note one thing you accomplished and one thing you learned. On confirmation day, you will have 90 entries of evidence.
  1. At the halfway point of your probation (Day 45 if it is 90 days), request a brief check-in with your manager: 'I want to make sure I am on track. Is there anything I should adjust?' This single conversation can save your job.

DECISION SCENARIO

You are six weeks into a three-month probation at a garment export company in Hlaing Tharyar. Your manager has not given you any feedback. A colleague tells you that your manager mentioned to HR that your reports have formatting errors. You were never told about a specific format. What do you do?

Option A: Wait for your manager to formally raise the issue with you, since correcting something you were never told about feels unfair.
Option B: Immediately ask your manager: 'I want to make sure my reports meet your expectations. Could you show me an example of the format you prefer?'
Option C: Ask your colleague to quietly share the correct format so you can fix it without drawing attention to the issue.
Option D: Start copying the format from a coworker's old reports and hope it matches what the manager wants.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Probation is not something that happens to you -- it is something you manage. The person who asks 'Am I on track?' in Week 3 is always in a stronger position than the person who waits silently until Week 12 and hopes for the best.
