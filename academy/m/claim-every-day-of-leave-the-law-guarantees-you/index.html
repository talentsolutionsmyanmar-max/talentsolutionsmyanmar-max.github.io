The Story

Ko Aung Kyaw had worked at a construction materials warehouse in Insein for three years. In all that time, he took exactly four days off — two when his father was hospitalized and two during Thingyan. Each time, his supervisor docked his pay. Ko Aung Kyaw never questioned it. He assumed leave was a privilege the employer could grant or deny. When his daughter got sick with dengue fever and he needed a full week away, the supervisor threatened to replace him. Ko Aung Kyaw went to work anyway, leaving his wife alone at the hospital. His coworker, Ma Phyu, told him afterward that Myanmar law guarantees 10 days of paid annual leave, 6 days of casual leave, and 30 days of medical leave per year. Ko Aung Kyaw had lost at least 48 days of paid leave over three years — and the pay deductions for the days he did take were illegal. He calculated the lost wages at over 400,000 kyats. But worse than the money was the week he was not at his daughter's hospital bed because he believed he had no choice.

The common mistakeCard 3 of 12
The Common Trap

Myanmar workers treat leave as something they must beg for and feel guilty about using, often working through illness and family emergencies without ever requesting their legal entitlement. The cognitive distortion is Mind Reading — workers assume 'If I ask for leave, my boss will think I am lazy and fire me' without any evidence this will happen. The cultural root cause is the loyalty-performance equation in Myanmar workplaces: being physically present is equated with being a good worker, and absence is equated with disloyalty regardless of the reason. The devastating consequence extends beyond lost wages — workers burn out, ignore health conditions until they become emergencies, and miss irreplaceable family moments, all while the law explicitly protects their right to be absent.

The teachingCard 4 of 12
Your Leave Entitlements Under Myanmar Law Are Non-Negotiable

Myanmar labor law provides three statutory leave categories that apply to all registered workers. Annual Leave: 10 consecutive days of paid leave per year for workers who have completed 12 months of continuous service. Casual Leave: 6 days per year for personal matters, taken in shorter periods. Medical Leave: 30 days per year, supported by a medical certificate for periods longer than 3 consecutive days. Additionally, all workers are entitled to paid public holidays as gazetted by the government — typically 20 or more days per year. These are not suggestions. These are legal minimums. Your employer cannot reduce them, and unused annual leave should carry forward or be compensated. Here is the behavioral shift that matters most: stop treating leave as a reward for being a good worker. Leave is compensation you have already earned through your labor, the same way your salary is. You would never voluntarily give back part of your salary each month. Yet that is exactly what you do when you forfeit leave days. The process of tracking and requesting your leave is the behavior to celebrate — not waiting to see if you get 'lucky' and your boss is in a generous mood.

The teachingCard 5 of 12
Requesting Leave in Myanmar Without Damaging Relationships

The practical challenge is not the law — it is the unspoken workplace culture that punishes leave-takers. Here is a strategy that works within Myanmar's relationship-first, hierarchy-respecting environment. First, submit written requests in advance. Verbal requests disappear and give your manager plausible deniability. A Viber or email request creates a record and shows professionalism. Second, plan leave around business cycles. If your company's busiest period is quarter-end, request annual leave during slower weeks. This shows you are strategic, not selfish. Third, always propose coverage. When you request leave, name a colleague who has agreed to handle urgent matters. This removes your manager's biggest fear — that your absence creates a problem for them. Fourth, reference the law gently. You do not need to quote statutes aggressively. Simply saying 'I am planning to use some of my annual leave entitlement' normalizes what is legally guaranteed. At companies like an established Myanmar bank and a major conglomerate, leave management is systematic with digital approval processes. Even if your current employer is less organized, behaving as if professional leave management is normal gradually raises the standard for everyone around you.

Action 1

Open your phone notes right now and create a 'My Leave Tracker.' Write three rows: (1) Annual Leave: 10 days per year, (2) Casual Leave: 6 days per year, (3) Medical Leave: 30 days per year with medical certificate. Below that, write your current year and mark how many days you have taken in each category. Calculate how many days remain. If you discover you have unused leave, you now have specific knowledge to plan with. Update this tracker every time you take leave.

Action 2

Take a photo of any leave policy document your company has given you — employee handbook, orientation papers, HR notice. If you have never received one, that is important information. Open Messenger or Viber and draft this message to HR: 'Mingalabar, could you kindly share our company leave policy so I can plan my leave properly for the year? I want to make sure I submit requests with enough advance notice. Thank you.' This frames your request as responsible planning, not demanding rights.

Do thisCard 8 of 12
Action 3

Open Google Calendar or the calendar app on your Android phone. Block out the public holidays for Myanmar 2026 — Thingyan, Independence Day, Union Day, Full Moon days, and other gazette holidays. These are paid days off by law. If your employer requires you to work on these days, they must provide compensatory leave or overtime pay. Having these dates marked makes it impossible for you to accidentally miss an entitlement you did not track.

Action 5

Draft a template leave request in your phone notes that you can modify for any future situation. Write: 'Dear [Manager Name], I would like to request [number] days of [annual/casual/medical] leave from [date] to [date]. As per the Shops and Establishments Law, I am entitled to [10 annual / 6 casual / 30 medical] days of leave per year. Having this template ready means you will never freeze when you need to request leave urgently.

Keep thisCard 11 of 12
Key Takeaway

Leave is not your employer's gift to you — it is your legal entitlement that your employer is required to provide. Every day of leave you fail to take is a benefit you earned but donated back to a company that is not legally allowed to refuse it.
