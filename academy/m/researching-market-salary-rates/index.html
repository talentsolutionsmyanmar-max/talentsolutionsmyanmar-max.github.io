CORE INSIGHT

Your salary is not decided by how hard you work. It is decided by how replaceable your employer believes you are -- and how well-informed you are when the number gets discussed. The single most powerful thing you can do before any salary conversation is know the range. Not a single number. A range: the low end, the midpoint, and the high end for your job title, your experience level, your industry, and your city.

Most Myanmar professionals skip this step entirely. They either accept whatever is offered because they feel grateful for the job, or they pick a number based on what their friends earn -- which may be in a completely different industry, company type, or city. The result is the same: you negotiate blind, and blind negotiators lose every time.

Here is what actually works. You gather salary data from at least three different sources before any negotiation or job change. You look for patterns, not perfection. If three sources suggest mid-level accountants in Yangon earn between 400,000 and 700,000 MMK, you now have power. You know where you stand. You know what to ask for. You know when an offer is genuinely low versus when your expectations are unrealistic.

THE MYANMAR REALITY

Salary data in Myanmar is harder to find than in Singapore or Bangkok, but it is not impossible. Here are real sources available to you right now in 2026:

Myanmar recruitment agencies like ReferTRM, JobNet, and others publish salary guides -- some free, some behind a form. MNC employers like Golden Beverage Co., Jade Ride, and a major telecom operator-successor companies tend to benchmark against regional standards, so their ranges run 30-80% above equivalent local company roles. NGOs and INGOs publish salary scales openly, especially UN agencies. Local companies vary wildly: a local conglomerate like Capital Diamond Star Group pays differently from a family-owned trading company with 15 staff.

The Yangon versus Mandalay gap is real. A senior accountant role paying 800,000 MMK in Yangon may pay 500,000-600,000 MMK in Mandalay. Do not use Yangon numbers for other cities.

In 2026, AI tools change this game. You can now use ChatGPT or similar tools to summarize publicly available salary data for Myanmar roles. You can ask: 'What is the typical salary range for a mid-level marketing manager in Yangon in 2026 across local companies, NGOs, and MNCs?' The answer will not be perfect, but it gives you a starting framework that is far better than guessing.

Gender matters here. Women in Myanmar are statistically less likely to negotiate salary and more likely to accept the first offer. If you are a woman reading this, the data you gather is your equalizer. Numbers do not care about cultural expectations. When you say 'the market range for this role is 500,000 to 650,000 and I am requesting 580,000,' the conversation shifts from personal to professional.

Real-World Scenario

Ko Naing went into a salary negotiation at a fintech company and asked for 500,000 MMK monthly. The hiring manager immediately agreed. Ko Naing felt proud — until he discovered his colleague with the same role and less experience was earning 750,000 MMK. The difference: his colleague had researched the market rate before negotiating. Ko Naing had guessed.

## Common Mistake

Most Myanmar professionals rely on one data point — what their friends earn — to determine their market value. But friend circles tend to be in similar industries and career stages, which creates a narrow and often inaccurate picture. The market rate for a senior accountant varies from 400,000 to 1,200,000 MMK depending on the company type (local SME vs. MNC vs. INGO), industry, and location. Without real data, you are negotiating blind.

## Action Steps

  1. Collect 5 salary data points for your target role: 2 from job postings (many now list ranges), 2 from people in similar roles (ask carefully: "What range do companies typically offer for this role?"), and 1 from a recruiter. The overlap is your market range.
  1. Check Myanmar job sites (JobNet, MyJobs, ReferTRM) for posted salary ranges in your field. Even if ranges are broad, they establish a floor and ceiling.
  1. When a recruiter asks your expected salary, always give a range — not a single number. "Based on my research, this role typically pays between X and Y" is stronger than "I want X."

## Reflection Question

Write down your current salary. Now write down what you believe the market rate is for your role and experience level. If you cannot write the market rate with confidence, you need to do more research before your next negotiation.
