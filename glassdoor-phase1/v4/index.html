Two kinds of truth

Applying is not working. The two are never mixed.

A story about how a company hires and a story about how a company treats its workers are different claims. Each carries its own proof, its own rules, and its own label — so one can never borrow the other's credibility.

Contacted via ReferTRM

A real, timestamped contact.

The person applied or was contacted through this platform, and the moment is on record. This proves an interaction happened.

It does not prove every word in the account is true. It does not prove the person ever worked there.

Self-reported

An open submission, held to a stricter rule.

A worker tells their own experience in their own words. Because it claims an employment relationship, it is held until that relationship is proven — and until publishing cannot identify the person.

Aggregated counts arrive first. Individual stories publish only when small numbers cannot expose a name, a team, or a season.

Your protection

Evidence must protect workers, not expose them.

A review site that gets a worker fired is not a truth site. Everything this surface shows — and everything it refuses to show — follows from that.

01 · No PII, ever

Your name is never the evidence.

No seeker identity is public. Verification records that a contact happened, not who you are.

02 · Empty until earned

Reviews stay empty until the path is governed.

Consent, eligibility, moderation, aggregation and a lifecycle exist first. No stars appear from silence.

03 · Aggregation before accounts

Small numbers can identify a person.

Counts publish before stories. A single account in a small team names its author — so it waits.

04 · Never shown to the employer

The company never sees who spoke.

Identity is shielded from the employer side by design, not by promise.

06

Before you apply

Protect the person who depends on that income.

01

Check the employer.

Use the exact registered or trading name and an official public source. A claim is not a verification.

02

Pay nothing to apply.

No registration fee, interview fee, placement fee, uniform deposit, or processing payment. Not ever.

03

Get the work in writing.

Salary, hours, location, reporting line, probation, leave — confirmed in writing before you accept.

The one warning that matters even with zero data.
A legitimate employer never asks an applicant for money. If any company asks you for a fee, a deposit, or a "training payment", treat it as a danger sign — and report it.
07

For employers

Free to begin. Verified to publish.

Posting begins with proof and stays free. Verification protects jobseekers; it never turns a posting organisation into a contracted client, and it never opens candidate data.

01

Start the request

The exact company name and an official public source. Identity first, always.

02

Verify employer control

A posting request and a verified status remain separate states until proof completes.

03

Review the job terms

Identity, role terms and worker-safety information must be clear before publication.
